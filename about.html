This site is built on top of data obtained from the following sources. We are deeply grateful to them.

advaitasharada.sringeri.net

Dakshinamnaya Sri Sharada Peetham, Sringeri — digital texts of the Śaṅkara-bhāṣyas, Ānandagiri's ṭīkās, Sureśvara's vārttikas and other commentaries hosted here.

Mahesh Research Institute — Dakshinamurti Math Publications

The printed Upaniṣadbhāṣyam is our base edition: the reference against which typos in the digital texts are corrected, and the source of the printed pratīka marks.

Their Prakaraṇadvādaśī (ed. S. Subrahmanya Shastri, 2nd edition, 2004; a free download from the Mahesh Research Institute) is the source of Padmapāda's Vedāntasāra ṭīkā on the Ātmabodha shown here.

Shri Kailash Ashram, Rishikesh — Shri Kailash Vidya Prakashan

Their Upaniṣad editions — the Īśāvāsya, Kena (with Śaṅkara's pada- and vākya-bhāṣya and Ānandagiri's ṭīkās; Śrīkailāsavidyāloka series no. 36; ed. Dr. Umeshananda Shastri), Kaṭha, Praśna, Muṇḍaka, Māṇḍūkya (with Gauḍapāda's kārikās), Taittirīya, Aitareya, Chāndogya and Bṛhadāraṇyaka, each with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya and Ānandagiri's ṭīkā — are the source of the गोविन्दप्रसादिनी lane on the pages of these ten Upaniṣads: the notes (ṭippaṇī) of Mahamandaleshwar Swami Vishnudevananda Giri Ji Maharaj on words of the bhāṣya and of Ānandagiri's ṭīkā. We read the notes from the printed pages and link each one to the word it explains; where the edition's text at that word differs from ours, the note is marked ⚑ with both readings. Each note is pinned to the word it explains in the mūla, bhāṣya or ṭīkā: a model locates the printed superscripts on the scanned page, and we number them in the edition's reading order, anchored by the notes' own quoted words and by placements checked on the scans. About 95% of the notes are pinned (on the Kena and Kaṭha pages every note, each checked against the print); the rest are shown under their verse, marked ○. Method: TIPPANI-METHODOLOGY.md. The notes are © Shri Kailash Ashram, Rishikesh.

Sri Tamal Maharaj — RKMVERI

The word-level digital reader of the Prasthānatrayī with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya (deposit, CC BY 4.0; paper arXiv:2607.07282): the bhāṣya word analysis behind the word cards. His Vedānta reader is the source of the padaccheda and word glosses of Ānandagiri's ṭīkās, and the padaccheda of Sureśvara's vārttikas and the Vaiyāsika-nyāya-mālā, shown here (where not our own). His word-by-word Gītā readers with Śrīdhara's Subodhinī and Madhusūdana's Gūḍhārthadīpikā are the source of those two Gītā commentaries here — his corrected text, with what the reading edition dropped restored from GRETIL or the printed edition — and of their word glosses. His Śaṅkara reader is the source of Acyutakṛṣṇānanda Tīrtha's Vanamālā on the Taittirīya-bhāṣya here — its text, its alignment to the bhāṣya, and its word glosses; its pratīkas are his marks together with the bold of the Mahesh Research Institute print. The same reader is the source of Govindānanda's Ratnaprabhā on the Brahmasūtra-bhāṣya and of Pūrṇānanda Sarasvatī's Pūrṇānandī on the Ratnaprabhā (shown as fold-outs under the Ratnaprabhā notes they explain), with their alignment and word glosses; pāṭhāntaras his e-text records are marked ⚑. It is also the source of the Sarvavedāntasiddhāntasārasaṅgraha here — its text (the Śṛṅgerī digital edition) and the padaccheda, grammar and meaning of every word. His Upaniṣad reader gave the mantra numbering of our Śvetāśvatara, and its text was one of those we checked the mūla against. And it is the source of the Dṛg-dṛśya-viveka here — its text and the padaccheda, grammar and meaning of every word; where the e-text differs from all three texts we checked it against (below), their reading is shown (listed on the page).

Internet Archive — Sri Ramakrishna Asrama, Mysore

The scan of Swami Nikhilananda's Dṛg-Dṛśya-Viveka (Sri Ramakrishna Asrama, Mysore, 1934): one of the three texts our Dṛg-dṛśya-viveka was checked against, verse by verse.

Vākyasudhā with Brahmānanda Bhāratī's ṭīkā — scanned print

A scanned print of the Vākyasudhā (Dṛg-dṛśya-viveka) with Brahmānanda Bhāratī's commentary (no publisher or year is printed in the scan): the base of our वाक्यसुधाटीका text, which we read from the scan, with its footnotes.

Arsha Avinash Foundation — Swami Paramarthananda

The section headings of the Dṛg-dṛśya-viveka follow the five parts of the text in Swami Paramarthananda's classes on it (free e-book from the Arsha Avinash Foundation), each divided further where the text turns to a new subject; wording ours. The verse text in that book (edited 2024) is one of the three texts our Dṛg-dṛśya-viveka was checked against.

The Quintessence of Vedanta of Sri Sankaracharya — Sri Ramakrishna Advaita Ashrama, Kalady

The section headings of the Sarvavedāntasiddhāntasārasaṅgraha are adapted from the contents of this translation (Swami Tattwananda, revised by Swami Jagadananda; Ramakrishna Math & Mission Publications), reworded.

Gita Supersite — IIT Kanpur

The digital texts of Nīlakaṇṭha's Bhāvadīpa and of Dhanapati Sūri's Bhāṣyotkarṣadīpikā on the Gītā (and the base of Sri Tamal Maharaj's Śrīdhara and Madhusūdana texts). Suspected misprints in the e-text are shown as printed and marked ⚑.

Internet Archive — eGangotri

The scan of the Nirṇayasāgara Press edition (1916) of Śaṅkarānanda's Gītātātparyabodhinī, from the collection of the Kashmiri Sanskrit scholar B. N. Kalla: the base of our Śaṅkarānandī text, which we read from the scan. The Bhole Baba edition (scan) was used to check it.

Ānandāśrama Sanskrit Series — Śvetāśvatara

The Ānandāśrama edition (Sanskrit Series no. 17, 1927) of the Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad with the bhāṣya ascribed to Śaṅkara (public domain): the base of our Śvetāśvatara mūla and bhāṣya, which we read from the scan, with its editor's manuscript readings shown as «पाठभेदाः». The Gītā Press edition (scan) was used to check it; printer's errors are kept as printed.

Internet Archive — Folkscanomy

The scan of the Nirṇayasāgara Press edition (1910) of the Ekādaśopaniṣadaḥ with Svāmī Amaradāsa Udāsīna's Maṇiprabhā and Nityānandāśrama's Mitākṣarā on the Chāndogya and Bṛhadāraṇyaka (public domain): the base of our Maṇiprabhā and Mitākṣarā texts, which we read from the scan. Two pages missing from that scan and a few illegible ones are supplied from a later re-typeset edition (scan) and marked ⚑; printer's errors are kept as printed and marked ⚑.
