📱 Use it as an app

You can put अध्ययन-सहकारी on your phone's or tablet's home screen. It then opens full-screen with its own icon, like an app. It is the same website: nothing comes from an app store, and it updates itself.

iPhone · iPad

Step 1 — open the link in Safari itself

A link tapped inside WhatsApp, Mail or another app opens in that app's built-in browser, which cannot add it to the Home Screen.

  1. Tap the Safari icon (a compass) or the ⋯ menu, usually at the top-right or bottom-right of the screen.
  2. Choose Open in Safari.

Step 2 — add it to your Home Screen

  1. In Safari, tap Share (a square with an arrow pointing up) in the address bar or toolbar. On recent iOS versions, tap ⋯ first if you don't see Share.
  2. Scroll down and tap Add to Home Screen. If it isn't there, scroll to the very bottom, tap Edit Actions, and turn it on.
  3. Keep or change the name, leave Open as Web App switched on, and tap Add.

Chrome and Edge on iOS 16.4 and later offer the same option through their Share button.

Android

  1. If the link opened inside WhatsApp or another app, tap ⋮ → Open in Chrome.
  2. In Chrome, tap ⋮ → Install app (on some phones: Add to Home screen → Install).

Updates — no reinstall needed

The app always fetches the latest version when you are online. If something looks out of date, close the app fully and open it again. Never delete and reinstall the app to get an update — on iPhone and iPad that erases your bookmarks and notes (see below).

Your bookmarks, highlights and notes

  • They are saved on your device and stay there across app restarts and updates. Nothing is sent to us (privacy).
  • iPhone · iPad: the Home-Screen app keeps its own library, separate from Safari's. Bookmarks made in Safari don't appear in the app, and the reverse. Deleting the app's icon deletes its library.
  • Android: the installed app shares Chrome's library.

To keep a backup, or move your library

Safari → app, old device → new, or before removing the app:

  1. Open ★ Library → Backup → Export. On iPhone or iPad, choose Save to Files (or AirDrop / Mail it to yourself).
  2. Where you want it, open ★ Library → Backup → Import and pick that file. Nothing already there is deleted; if the same item exists in both, the more recently edited copy is kept.

Export every now and then — it is your only backup.

Without a connection

To read a text anywhere — on a flight, with no signal — open any of its pages while online and choose ☰ → ⤓ Save this text for offline. A short list shows what will be kept and how much space each part takes: the text itself (with the commentaries shown in its pages), each commentary that loads separately (for the Gītā: श्रीधरी, नीलकण्ठी, मधुसूदनी, शङ्करानन्दी, भाष्योत्कर्षदीपिका), and Search within this text. All are ticked; untick what you don't need. Saved texts stay up to date on their own: whenever you are online, only what changed is fetched again.

  • Pages you have simply opened are also kept, so a page you read recently usually opens offline too.
  • See, change or remove saved texts in ★ My library → Offline (or ☰ → ✓ Saved for offline on the text).
  • Search within a saved text works offline if you kept Search within this text. Searching all texts, and the dictionary, need a connection.

Reading mode — just the text

On a phone, after choosing the chapter and verse, tap the four-corners button in the header (next to ☰). The header goes away and the text fills the screen. Tapping a word still shows its card. To get the header back — to search, change verse or open ☰ — tap the small round button at the bottom right, just above ⚑ report an issue. A page always opens with the header showing.
