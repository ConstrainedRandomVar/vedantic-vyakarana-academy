1.2.1

1.2.2
श्रेयश्च प्रेयश्च मनुष्यमेतस्तौ सम्परीत्य विविनक्ति धीरः श्रेयो हि धीरोऽभि प्रेयसो वृणीते प्रेयो मन्दो योगक्षेमाद्वृणीते
the good (the more beneficial); and the pleasant (the merely agreeable); and the two (good and pleasant) come to / reach the man; those two (= श्रेयःप्रेयस्, object of the next verbs) having fully approached, examined them thoroughly in mind separates, discriminates (sets the two apart, weighing their gravity and lightness) the wise (intelligent) one the good (object chosen) indeed (emphatic: श्रेयस् alone) the wise one; (chooses) in preference / above than the pleasant (śreyas being more honoured than it) chooses (in preference) the pleasant (object chosen) the dull-witted (feeble-minded) one chooses (the pleasant) for the sake of acquisition-and-preservation (the upkeep and protection of the body, etc.)

1.2.3
स त्वं प्रियान्प्रियरूपांश्च कामानभिध्यायन्नचिकेतोऽत्यस्राक्षीः नैतां सृङ्कां वित्तमयीमवाप्तो यस्यां मज्जन्ति बहवो मनुष्याः
that (you) — in apposition with त्वम्, addressing Naciketas you (Naciketas), though repeatedly tempted by me (Yama) the dear ones (sons and the like) and the pleasing-formed (nymphs and the like) reflecting on the desires (i.e. on their faults of impermanence and insubstantiality), O Naciketas, you have cast (them) off not this (path, agreeing with सृङ्काम्) the despicable course/path (followed by deluded people) consisting of wealth — (you have) not attained (it) in which (path/course) they sink / perish (सीदन्ति) many (अनेके) (deluded) men

1.2.4
दूरमेते विपरीते विषूची अविद्या या च विद्येति ज्ञाता विद्याभीप्सिनं नचिकेतसं मन्ये न त्वा कामा बहवोऽलोलुपन्त
far apart (by a great distance) — these two (vidyā and avidyā) (the two are) mutually opposed, contrary going apart in different directions, with differing fruits ignorance (the realm of the pleasant, preyas) which (relative, qualifying avidyā) and knowledge — thus (the realm of the good, śreyas) (the two are) known, discerned by the wise (paṇḍitaiḥ) desirous of knowledge, seeker of vidyā Naciketas (object of 'I deem') I deem, I consider not you (Naciketas) desires (the many objects of pleasure, apsaras etc.) many (desires) did not tear (you) away (na vicchedaṃ kṛtavantaḥ)

1.2.6
