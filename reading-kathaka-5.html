2.2.1
पुरमेकादशद्वारमजस्यावक्रचेतसः अनुष्ठाय न शोचति विमुक्तश्च विमुच्यते एतद्वै तत्
the eleven-gated city (= the body, śarīraṃ puram) belonging to the unborn (aja, free of birth and other modifications) Self of unbent/unwavering consciousness (its knowing ever one, steady like the sun's light) having practised/realised — i.e. having meditated upon (the Lord of that city); bhāṣya: anuṣṭhāya = dhyātvā, dhyānaṃ hi tasyānuṣṭhānam not he grieves — (na śocati: having no occasion for sorrow, since knowledge of That brings fearlessness) and [already] freed — released from all desires (sarvaiṣaṇā-vinirmuktaḥ) while living he is [finally/fully] liberated (videha-mukti, on the falling away of the body) this (the inmost Self here realised) verily is That (the supreme Brahman asked about) — the refrain 'This verily is That'

हंसः शुचिषद्वसुरन्तरिक्षसद्धोता वेदिषदतिथिर्दुरोणसत् नृषद्वरसदृतसद्व्योमसदब्जा गोजा ऋतजा अद्रिजा ऋतं बृहत्

2.2.5
न प्राणेन नापानेन मर्त्यो जीवति कश्चन इतरेण तु जीवन्ति यस्मिन्नेतावुपाश्रितौ
not by the out-breath (prāṇa) nor by the in-breath (apāna) a mortal, an embodied man (मनुष्यो देहवान्) lives, stays alive (with मर्त्यः: no mortal lives) anyone whatsoever (न कोऽपि — none at all) by the other — i.e. the Self, distinct from the aggregated prāṇa etc. (संहतप्राणादिविलक्षणेन) but, rather (emphasising the contrast) they (all the aggregated beings) live and sustain their breaths (सर्वे ... जीवन्ति प्राणान् धारयन्ति) in whom (the Self) these two — prāṇa and apāna (together with eye etc.) — are dependent and take their refuge (संहतौ उपाश्रितौ)
