12.1
ननु एवम् वासना-आनन्दात् ब्रह्म-आनन्दात् अपि इतरम् वेत्तु योगी निज-आनन्दम् मूढस्य अत्र अस्ति का गतिः
Surely, thus let the yogi know his own bliss, different from impression-bliss and Brahman-bliss. What recourse is there here for the ignorant person?
12.2
धर्म-अधर्म-वशात् एषः जायताम् म्रियताम् अपि पुनः पुनः देह-लक्षैः किम् नः दाक्षिण्यतः वद
Let this one be born by virtue and vice, and let him die again and again through lakhs of bodies. Tell, what is it to us out of compassion?
12.3
अस्ति वः अनुजिघृक्षुत्वात् दाक्षिण्येन प्रयोजनम् तर्हि ब्रूहि सः मूढः किम् जिज्ञासुः वा पराङ्मुखः
If you have a need for compassion owing to a desire to show grace, then tell, is that ignorant person desirous of knowledge or averse?
12.4
उपास्तिम् कर्म वा ब्रूयात् विमुखाय यथा-उचितम् मन्द-प्रज्ञम् तु जिज्ञासुम् आत्म-आनन्देन बोधयेत्
One should teach worship or ritual action as appropriate to the averse person. But one should enlighten the dull-witted seeker after truth with the bliss of the Self.
12.5
बोधयामास मैत्रेयीम् याज्ञवल्क्यः निज-प्रियाम् न वै अरे पत्युः अर्थे पतिः प्रियः इति ईरयन्
Yājñavalkya taught his beloved wife Maitreyī, declaring... 'Indeed, O dear one, a husband is not dear for the sake of the husband...'
12.6
पतिः जाया पुत्र-वि्त्ते पशु-ब्राह्मण-बाहुजाः लोकाः देवाः वेद-भूते सर्वम् च आत्म-अर्थतः प्रियम्
Husband, wife, children and wealth, cattle, brahmins and kshatriyas, worlds, gods, Vedas and creatures—everything is dear for the sake of the Self.
12.7
पत्याव इच्छा यदा पत्न्याः तदा प्रीतिम् करोति सा क्षुत्-अनुष्ठान-रोग-आद्यैः तदा न इच्छति तत्-पतिः
When there is desire for the husband on the part of the wife, then she shows affection. At that time (due to) hunger, performance of duties, illness etc., her husband does not desire her.
12.8
न पत्युः अर्थे सा प्रीतिः स्व-अर्थे एव करोति ताम् पतिः च आत्मनः एव अर्थे न जाया-अर्थे कदाचन अन्योन्य-प्रेरणे अपि एवम् स्व-इच्छया एव प्रवर्तनम्
That affection is not for the sake of the husband; she shows it for her own sake alone. And the husband acts for his own sake only, never for the sake of the wife. Even in mutual prompting, action is thus out of one's own desire alone.
12.9
श्मश्रु-कण्टक-वेधेन बालः रुदति तत्-पिता चुम्बति एव न सा प्रीतिः बाल-अर्थे स्व-अर्थे एव सा
When a child cries from the prick of a rough beard, his father still kisses him. That affection is not for the child's sake; it is for his own pleasure alone.
12.10
निरिच्छम् अपि रत्न-आदि वित्तम् यत्नेन पालयन् प्रीतिम् करोति सा स्व-अर्थे वित्त-अर्थत्वम् न शङ्कितम्
Guarding wealth such as gems with effort though it is desireless, one cherishes it. That love is for self-interest; being for the sake of wealth is not even suspected.
12.11
अनिच्छति बलीवर्दे विवाहयिषते बलात् प्रीतिः सा वणिग्-अर्था एव बलीवर्द-अर्थता कुतः
Even when the bullock is unwilling, (the merchant) makes it carry load by force. That care is solely for the merchant's gain. How could it be for the bullock's sake?
12.12
ब्राह्मण्यम् मे अस्ति पूज्यः अहम् इति तुष्यति पूजया अचेतनायाः जातेः नो सन्तुष्टिः पुंसः एव सा
'Brahminhood is mine, I am worshipful!' Thinking thus, one rejoices in worship. That satisfaction belongs to the person alone, not to the insentient caste.
12.13
क्षत्रियः अहम् तेन राज्यम् करोमि इति अत्र राजता न जातेः वैश्य-जाति-आदौ योजनाय इदम् ईरितम्
'I am a Kshatriya, therefore I rule...' Here kingship is felt, not by the caste itself. This is stated for applying the same logic to Vaishya caste and others.
12.14
स्वर्गलोक-ब्रह्मलोकौ स्याताम् मम इति अभिवाञ्छनम् लोकयोः न उपकाराय स्व-भोगाय एव केवलम्
'May Svarga and Brahmaloka be mine!' Desiring thus is not for the benefit of those two worlds, but solely for one's own enjoyment.
12.15
ईश-विष्णु-आदयः देवाः पूज्यन्ते पाप-नष्टये न तत्-निष्पाप-देव-अर्थम् स्व-अर्थम् तत् तु उपयुज्यते
The gods like Śiva and Viṣṇu are worshipped for the destruction of sins. That worship is not performed for the sake of those sinless gods, but serves one's own interest.
12.16
ऋग्-आदयः हि अधीयन्ते दुर्ब्राह्मण्य-अनवाप्तये न तत् प्रसक्तम् वेदेषु मनुष्येषु प्रसज्यते
For the Vedas such as Ṛgveda are studied to avoid becoming bad brahmins. That obligation does not reside in the Vedas themselves, it attaches to human beings.
12.17
भूमि-आदि-पञ्च-भूतानि स्थान-तृट्-पाक-शोषणैः हेतुभिः च अवकाशेन वाञ्छन्ति एषाम् न हेतवे
People desire the five elements starting with earth for support, quenching thirst, cooking, drying and space, not for the elements' own sake.
12.18
स्वामि-भृत्य-आदिकम् सर्वम् स्व-उपकाराय वाञ्छति तत्-तत्-कृत-उपकारः तु तस्य तस्य न विद्यते
Everyone desires all master-servant relations for their own benefit. However, the service done by each is not for the sake of that other.
12.19
सर्व-व्यवहृतिषु एवम् अनुसन्धातुम् ईदृशम् उदाहरण-बाहुल्यम् तेन स्वाम् वासयेत् मतिम
There is a abundance of examples to examine such truth in all dealings. Through this, one should condition one's mind.
12.20
अथ का इयम् भवेत् प्रीतिः श्रूयते या निज-आत्मनि रागः वध्वादि-विषये श्रद्धा यागादि-कर्मणि भक्तिः स्यात् गुरु-देव-आदौ इच्छा तु अप्राप्त-वस्तुनि
Now, what might this love be? which is heard of regarding one's own Self? Passion is towards object like a wife; faith is towards actions such as sacrifices; devotion is towards Guru, God, and others; while desire is towards an unobtained object.
12.21
तर्हि अस्तु सात्त्विकी वृत्तिः सुख-मात्र-अनुवर्तिनी प्राप्ते नष्टे अपि सद्भावात् इच्छतः व्यतिरिच्यते
Let the sattvic mental state be following happiness only. Because of existence when [an object is] obtained or lost, it excels for the desirer.
12.22
सुख-साधनता-उपाधेः अन्न-पान-आदयः प्रियाः आत्म-आनुकूल्यात् अन्न-आदि-समः चेत् अमुना अत्र कः अनुकूलयितव्यः स्यात् न एकस्मिन् कर्म-कर्तृ-ता
Food, drink, etc., are dear because of their qualification as means to happiness. If [the Self] were equal to food etc., owing to being favorable to oneself, then who here would be made favorable by this [Self]? For being both object and agent in a single entity is impossible.
12.23
सुखे वैषयिके प्रीति-मात्रम् आत्मा तु अति-प्रियः सुखे व्यभिचरति एषा न आत्मनि व्यभिचारिणी
For sensory pleasure, there is only mere affection; but the Self is supreme love. This love fluctuates with regard to sensual happiness, it is never variable regarding the Self.
12.24
एकम् त्यक्त्वा अन्यत् आदत्ते सुखम् वैषयिकम् सदा न आत्मा त्याज्यः न च आदेयः तस्मिन् व्यभिचरेत् कथम्
One always discards one sensory pleasure and acquires another. The Self is not to be abandoned, nor to be acquired. How then could love vary regarding it?
12.25
हान-आदान-विहीनः अस्मिन् उपेक्षा चेत् तृण-आदि-वत् उपेक्षितुः स्वरूपत्वात् न उपेक्ष्यत्वम् निज-आत्मनः
If there is indifference towards this (Self), which is free of giving up or taking, like a straw, there is no state of being ignored for one's own Self, since it is the essential nature of the ignorer.
12.26
रोग-क्रोध-अभिभूतानाम् मुमूर्षा वीक्ष्यते क्वचित् ततः द्वेषात् भवेत् त्याज्यः आत्मा इति यदि तत् न हि त्यक्तुम् योग्यस्य देहस्य न आत्मता त्यक्तुः एव सा न त्यक्तरि अस्ति सः द्वेषः त्याज्ये द्वेषे तु का क्षतिः
A desire for death is sometimes observed in people overcome by illness or anger. 'Therefore, out of hatred, the Self should be discarded' If that is said, indeed it is not so. Selfhood does not belong to the body, which is fit to be abandoned; that [selfhood] belongs only to the abandoner. That hatred does not exist in the abandoner. However, when there is hatred towards what is to be discarded, what harm is there?
12.27
आत्म-अर्थत्वेन सर्वस्य प्रीतेः च आत्मा हि अति-प्रियः यथा पितुः पुत्र-मित्रात् पुत्रः प्रिय-तरः तथा
And because love for everything exists for the sake of the Self, the Self is indeed most dear. Just as to a father, his own son is dearer than a son's friend.
12.28
मा न भूवम् अहम् किन्तु भूयासम् सर्वदा इति असौ आशीः सर्वस्य दृष्टा इति प्रत्यक्षा प्रीतिः आत्मनि
'May I never cease to exist!' 'But may I exist forever!' This prayer is seen in everyone. Therefore, love for the Self is directly evident.
12.29
इति-आदिभिः त्रिभिः प्रीतौ सिद्धायाम् एवम् आत्मनि पुत्र-भार्या-आदि-शेषत्वम् आत्मनः कैश्चित् ईरितम्
Love for the Self being thus established by these three reasons, the subordination of the Self to son, wife, etc., has been stated by some.
12.30
एतत्-विवक्षया पुत्रे मुख्य-आत्मत्वम् श्रुति-ईरितम् आत्मा वै पुत्र-नामा इति तत् च उपनिषदि स्फुटम्
With the intention of saying this, primary selfhood is declared to reside in the son by the Veda. 'You are indeed the Self named Son.' And that [statement] is clear in the Upanishad.
12.31
सः अस्य अयम् आत्मा पुण्येभ्यः कर्मेभ्यः प्रतिधीयते अथ अस्य इतरः आत्मा अयम् कृत-कृत्यः प्रमीयते
This son, as his self, is substituted for holy duties. Then his other self (the father), having accomplished his duties, passes away.
12.32
सति अपि आत्मनि लोकः अस्ति न अपुत्रस्य अतः एव हि अनुशिष्टम् पुत्रम् एव लोक्यम् आहुः मनीषिणः
Even though the Self exists, there is no world for one who has no son. Hence indeed the wise declare only an instructed son to be the securing of the world.
12.33
मनुष्य-लोकः जय्यः स्यात् पुत्रेण एव इतरेण नो मुमूर्षुः मन्त्रयेत् पुत्रम् त्वम् ब्रह्म इति-आदि-मन्त्रकैः
The world of men is to be won through a son only, not by any other means. 'You are Brahman!' The dying man should instruct his son with mantras such as 'You are Brahman'.
12.34
इति-आदि-श्रुतयः प्राहुः पुत्र-भार्या-आदि-शेषताम् लौकिकाः अपि पुत्रस्य प्राधान्यम् अनुमन्यन्ते
Such Sruti texts declare the secondary nature of the Self relative to son, wife, etc. Even worldly people accept the primacy of the son.
12.35
स्वस्मिन् मृते अपि पुत्र-आदिः जीवेत् वित्त-आदिना यथा तथा एव यत्नम् कुरुते मुख्याः पुत्र-आदयः ततः
So that even when oneself is dead, the son etc., may live through wealth etc., one makes effort accordingly; therefore, the son etc., are primary.
12.36
बाढम् एतावता न आत्मा शेषः भवति कस्यचित् गौण-मिथ्या-मुख्य-भेदैः आत्मा अयम् भवति त्रिधा
True, but by this much the Self does not become secondary to anyone. This Self is threefold according to the divisions of secondary, false, and primary.
12.37
देवदत्तः तु सिंहः अयम् इति ऐक्यम् गौणम् एतयोः भेदस्य भासमानत्वात् पुत्र-आदेः आत्मता तथा
'This Devadatta is a lion' The identity of these two in 'Devadatta is a lion' is figurative, because their difference is obvious. Similarly is the selfhood of son, etc.
12.38
भेदः अस्ति पंच-कोषेषु साक्षिणः न तु भाति असौ मिथ्या-आत्मता तः कोषाणाम् स्थाणोः चौर-आत्मता यथा
Difference from the Witness exists in the five sheaths, but that [difference] is not perceived. Hence the selfhood of the sheaths is illusory, just as a post is falsely taken for a thief.
12.39
न भाति भेदः न अपि अस्ति साक्षिणः अ-प्रतियोगिनः सर्व-अन्तरत्वात् तस्य एव मुख्यम् आत्मत्वम् इष्यते
No difference appears, nor indeed does any exist for the Witness who has no counterpart. Because it is innermost of all, primary selfhood is affirmed of that very Witness.
12.40
सति एवम् व्यवहारेषु येषु यस्य आत्मता उचिता तेषु तस्य एव शेषित्वम् सर्वस्य अन्यस्य शेषता
This being so, in whichever worldly transactions selfhood is appropriate for a particular entity, in those transactions, primacy belongs to that entity alone, while subordination belongs to everything else.
12.41
मुमूर्षोः गृह-रक्षा-आदौ गौण-आत्मा एव उपयुज्यते न मुख्य-आत्मा न मिथ्या-आत्मा पुत्रः शेषी भवति अतः
The secondary self alone is employed in household protection etc. of a dying person, not the primary self nor the illusory self. Therefore the son becomes the principal.
12.42
अध्येता वह्निः इति अत्र सन् अपि अग्निः न गृह्यते अयोग्यत्वेन योग्यत्वात् वटुः एव अत्र गृह्यते
'The student is fire' Here, fire, though present, is not taken because of unfitness. Because of fitness, the boy alone is taken here.
12.43
कृशः अहम् पुष्टिम् आप्स्यामि इति आदौ देह-आत्मता उचिता न पुत्रम् विनियुङ्क्ते अत्र पुष्टि-हेतु-अन्न-भक्षणे
'I am thin, I will gain strength' Body-self identity is appropriate in such statements. Here one does not appoint the son in eating food meant for nourishment.
12.44
तपसा स्वर्गम् एष्यामि इति आदौ कर्तृ-आत्मता उचिता अनपेक्ष्य वपुः-भोगम् चरेत् कृच्छ्र-आदिकम् ततः
'By austerity I shall go to heaven' In such cases agent-selfhood is appropriate. Therefore, disregarding physical comfort, one should perform penances.
12.45
मोक्ष्ये अहम् इति अत्र युक्तम् चित्-आत्मत्वम् तदा पुमान् तत् वेत्ति गुरु-शास्त्राभ्याम् न तु किंचित् चिकीर्षति
'I shall be liberated' In this statement, consciousness-selfhood is proper. Then a man knows that through guru and scriptures. But he does not desire to perform anything.
12.46
विप्र-क्षत्र-आदयः यद्वत् बृहस्पतिसव-आदिषु व्यवस्थिताः तथा गौण-मिथ्या-मुख्याः यथा-उचितम्
Just as Brāhmaṇas, Kṣatriyas, etc. are specified in rituals like Bṛhaspatisava, so too the secondary, false, and primary selves are designated as appropriate.
12.47
तत्र तत्र उचिते प्रीतिः आत्मनि एव अतिशायिनी अनात्मनि तु तत्-शेषे प्रीतिः अन्यत्र न उभयम्
In each appropriate context, supreme love is directed to the self alone. However, love for non-self is only because it is subsidiary to the self. Elsewhere, neither exists.
12.48
उपेक्ष्यम् द्वेष्यम् इति अन्यत् द्वेधा मार्ग-तृण-आदिकम् उपेक्ष्यम् व्याघ्र-सर्प-आदि द्वेष्यम् एवम् चतुर्विधम्
Other objects are of two kinds: indifferent and hateful. Straw on the path etc. is indifferent. Tigers, snakes, etc. are hateful. Thus the division is fourfold.
12.49
आत्मा शेषः उपेक्ष्यम् च द्वेष्यम् च इति चतुर्षु अपि न व्यक्ति-नियमः किन्तु तत्-तत्-कार्यात् तथा तथा
'Self, secondary, indifferent, and hateful' Even among these four categories, there is no fixed rule for individual entities. Rather, it varies accordingly based on the specific effect/action.
12.50
स्यात् व्याघ्रः संमुखः द्वेष्यः हि उपेक्ष्यः तु पराङ्मुखः लालनात् अनुकूलः चेत् विनोदाय इति शेषताम्
For a tiger facing one would be hateful. However, turning away, it would be indifferent. If through taming it becomes friendly, for entertainment it reaches the status of a subsidiary.
12.51
व्यक्तिनाम् नियमः मा भूत् लक्षणात् तु व्यवस्थितिः आनुकूल्यम् प्रातिकूल्यम् द्वय-अभावः च लक्षणम्
Let there be no fixed rule regarding individual entities. However, determination comes from definition. The defining mark is agreeableness, disagreeableness, and the absence of both.
12.52
आत्मा प्रेयान् प्रियः शेषः द्वेष्य-उपेक्ष्ये तत्-अन्ययोः इति व्यवस्थितः लोकः याज्ञवल्क्य-मतम् च तत्
'Self is dearest, subsidiary is dear, hateful and indifferent are the remaining two' The world is thus ordered. And that is Yājñavalkya's view.
12.53
अन्यत्र अपि श्रुतिः प्राह पुत्रात् वित्तात् तथा अन्यतः सर्वस्मात् आन्तरम् तत्त्वम् तत् एतत् प्रेयः ईक्षताम्
Elsewhere also Śruti declares: 'Dearer than son, wealth, and everything else is the inmost Truth; let one realize this as that.'
12.54
श्रौत्या विचार-दृष्ट्या अयम् साक्षी एव आत्मा न च इतरः कोषान् पञ्च विविच्य अन्तः-वस्तु-दृष्टिः विचारणा
By Vedic analytical vision, this Witness alone is Self, not the other. Inquiry is the vision of the inner reality after separating the five sheaths.
12.55
जागर-स्वप्न-सुप्तीनाम् आगम-अपाय-भासनम् यतः भवति असौ आत्मा स्वप्रकाश-चित्-आत्मकः
From which the illumination of the arrival and departure of waking, dream, and deep sleep occurs, that is the Self, self-luminous and consciousness-natured.
12.56
शेषाः प्राण-आदि-वित्त-अन्ताः आसन्नाः तारतम्यतः प्रीतिः तथा तारतम्यात् तेषु सर्वेषु वीक्ष्यते
The remaining items, starting from prāṇa and ending with wealth, are close in varying degrees. Likewise, love for all of them is seen in degrees.
12.57
वित्तात् पुत्रः प्रियः पुत्रात् पिण्डः पिण्डात् तथा इन्द्रियम् इन्द्रियात् च प्रियः प्राणः प्राणात् आत्मा परः प्रियः
Son is dearer than wealth. Body is dearer than a son. Likewise sense organs are dearer than the body. And life force is dearer than sense organs. The Self is the most supreme dear object, dearer than life force.
12.58
एवम् स्थिते विवादः अत्र प्रतिबुद्ध-विमूढयोः श्रुत्या उदाहारि तत्र आत्मा प्रेयान् इति एव निर्णयः
When things stand thus, the dispute here between the enlightened and the ignorant was cited by Śruti. 'Self is dearest' This alone is the decision there.
12.59
साक्षी एव दृश्यात् अन्यस्मात् प्रेयान् इति आह तत्त्ववित् प्रेयान् पुत्र-आदिः एव इमम् भोक्तुम् साक्षी इति मूढ-धीः
'The Witness alone is dearer than all other perceived objects' says the knower of Truth. 'Son etc. alone is dearer, the Witness is meant to enjoy this' so thinks the foolish person.
12.60
आत्मनः अन्यम् प्रियम् ब्रूते शिष्यः च प्रतिवादी अपि तस्य उत्तरम् वाचः बोध-शापौ कुर्यात् तयोः क्रमात्
Both the disciple and the opponent declare something other than the Self to be dear. As an answer to their statement, one should issue enlightenment and a curse to those two respectively.
12.61
प्रियम् त्वाम् रोत्स्यति इति एवम् उत्तरम् वक्ति तत्त्व-विद् स्व-उक्त-प्रियस्य दुष्टत्वम् शिष्यः वेत्ति विवेकतः
'[The son] will obstruct/cause pain to you who love him' The knower of truth gives such a reply The disciple understands through discrimination the defectiveness of what he considered dear
12.62
अलभ्यमानः तनयः पितरौ क्लेशयेत् चिरम् लब्धः अपि गर्भ-पातेन प्रसवेन च बाधते
A son not yet conceived distresses the parents for a long time Even when conceived, he torments them by miscarriage and painful delivery
12.63
जातस्य ग्रह-रोग-आदि कुमारस्य च मूकता उपनीते अपि अविद्यत्वम् अनुद्वाहः च पण्डिते
When born, there are afflictions/diseases and dumbness of the child Even when invested with sacred thread, there is ignorance And when he becomes learned, there is inability to marry
12.64
पुनः च पर-दार-आदि दारिद्र्यम् च कुटुम्बिनः पित्रोः दुःखस्य न अस्ति अन्तः धनी चेत् म्रियते तदा
And again, there is no end to the parents' sorrow due to adultery, poverty, etc., of the son If he dies after becoming rich then [great is their sorrow]
12.65
एवम् विविच्य पुत्र-आदौ प्रीतिम् त्यक्त्वा निज-आत्मनि निश्चित्य परमाम् प्रीतिम् वीक्षते तम् अहर्निशम्
Having thus discriminated and relinquished attachment to son etc., and having ascertained supreme love in one's own Self, he contemplates It day and night
12.66
आग्रहात् ब्रह्मविद्-द्वेषात् अपि पक्षम् अमुञ्चतः वादिनः नरकः प्रोक्तः दोषः च बहु-योनिषु
Hell and suffering in various rebirths are declared for the disputant who out of stubbornness or hatred of a knower of Brahman does not give up his dogma
12.67
ब्रह्मविद् ब्रह्म-रूपत्वात् ईश्वरः तेन वर्णितम् यत्-यत् तत्-तत् तथा एव स्यात् तत्-शिष्य-प्रतिवादिनोः
Because of being of the nature of Brahman, the knower of Brahman is Ishvara Whatever is uttered by him All that happens exactly so for his disciple and opponent
12.68
यः तु साक्षिणम् आत्मानम् सेवते प्रियम् उत्तमम् तस्य प्रेयान असौ आत्मा न नश्यति कदाचन
Whoever worships the Witness-Self as the supreme beloved His beloved Self never perishes at any time
12.69
पर-प्रेम-आस्पदत्वेन परम-आनन्द-रूपता सुख-वृद्धिः प्रीति-वृद्धौ सार्वभौम-आदिषु श्रुता
[Self's] state of being supreme bliss is due to its being the locus of supreme love Increase of happiness upon increase of love is declared in scripture regarding emperors and others
12.70
चैतन्यवत् सुखम् च अस्य स्वभावः चेत् चित्-आत्मनः धी-वृत्तिषु अनुवर्तेत सर्वासु अपि चितिः यथा
If bliss is the natural essence of this Conscious Self just like consciousness [then bliss] ought to persist in all mental modifications just as consciousness persists
12.71
मा एवम् उष्ण-प्रकाश-आत्मा दीपः तस्य प्रभा गृहे व्याप्नोति न उष्णता तद्वत् चितेः एव अनुवर्तनम्
Do not think in this manner Though a lamp possesses both heat and light as its nature, its light pervades the room [but its] heat does not [pervade the room] Just so, there is the persistence of consciousness alone [in all mental modes, not bliss]
12.72
गन्ध-रूप-रस-स्पर्शेषु अपि सत्सु यथा पृथक् एक-अक्षेण एकः एव अर्थः गृह्यते न इतरः तथा
Just as even when smell, color, taste, and touch exist together, only one quality is perceived by a single sense organ and not the other, so it is here
12.73
चित्-आनन्दौ न एव भिन्नौ गन्ध-आद्याः तु विलक्षणाः इति चेत् तत्-अभेदः अपि साक्षिणि अन्यत्र वा वद
If you say: Consciousness and bliss are not distinct at all, whereas smell etc. are distinct from each other Tell whether their non-difference is in the Witness or in the mind (vritti)!
12.74
आद्ये गन्ध-आदयः अपि एवम् अभिन्नाः पुष्प-वर्तिनः अक्ष-भेदेन तत्-भेदे वृत्ति-भेदात् तयोः भिदा
In the first case, smell etc. residing in a flower are likewise non-different When their difference occurs through the diversity of sense organs [in the second case], difference between the two arises due to difference of mental modifications
12.75
सत्त्व-वृत्तौ चित्-सुख-ऐक्यम् तत्-वृत्तेः निर्मलत्वतः रजः-वृत्तेः तु मालिन्यात् सुख-अंशः अत्र तिरस्कृतः
In a sattvic vritti, there is manifestation of unity of Consciousness and Bliss due to the purity of that vritti However, due to the impurity of a rajasic vritti, the aspect of bliss is veiled here
12.76
तिन्तिणी-फलम् अति-अम्लम् लवणेन युतम् यदा तदा अम्लस्य तिरस्कारात् ईषत्-अम्लम् यथा तथा
When tamarind fruit, which is extremely sour, is mixed with salt then due to partial suppression of sourness it becomes only mildly sour; just so here
12.77
ननु प्रियतमत्वेन परम-आनन्दता आत्मनि विवेक्तुम् शक्येत एवम् विना योगेन किम् भवेत्
Objection: Granted that supreme bliss in the Self can be discriminated because of its being most beloved What would be accomplished in this way without Yoga practice?
12.78
यत् योगेन तत् एव एति वदामः ज्ञान-सिद्धये योगः प्रोक्तः विवेकेन ज्ञानम् किम् न उपजायते
Whatever [goal] one attains by Yoga We say that very same goal is attained [by discrimination] Yoga is prescribed for achieving knowledge Why should knowledge not arise directly by discrimination?
12.79
यत् साङ्ख्यैः प्राप्यते स्थानम् तत् योगैः अपि गम्यते इति स्मृतम् फल-एकत्वम् योगिनाम् च विवेकिनाम्
The state which is attained by the Sankhyas that is attained by the Yogis also — thus the identity of fruit for Yogis and discriminative seekers is declared in the Smriti
12.80
असाध्यः कस्यचित् योगः कस्यचित् ज्ञान-निश्चयः इत्थम् विचार्य मार्गौ द्वौ जगाद परम-ईश्वरः
For someone Yoga is difficult/impossible for another, steady conviction in knowledge is difficult Having considered thus, the Supreme Lord taught the two paths
12.81
योगे कः अतिशयः ते अत्र ज्ञानम् उक्तम् समम् द्वयोः रागद्वेषाद्यभावः च तुल्यः योगिविवेकिनोः
What is the superiority of you in yoga? Knowledge is stated to be equal for the two. And the absence of attachment and aversion is equal for the yogin and the discriminator.
12.82
न प्रीतिः विषयेषु अस्ति प्रेयान् आत्मा इति जानतः कुतः रागः कुतः द्वेषः प्रातिकूल्यम् अपश्यतः
There is no delight in sense objects. of one knowing thus: 'The Self is dearer.' Whence is attachment? Whence is aversion for one not seeing adversity?
12.83
देहादेः प्रतिकूलेषु द्वेषः तुल्यः द्वयोः अपि द्वेषम् कुर्वन् योगी चेत् अविवेक्यपि तादृशः
Aversion toward unfavorable things of the body etc. is equal in both too. If the yogin exhibits aversion, even the non-discriminating person is like that.
12.84
द्वैतस्य प्रतिभानम् तु व्यवहारे द्वयोः समम् समाधौ न इति चेत् तत् वत् अद्वैतत्वविवेकिनः
However, the appearance of duality in empirical dealings is equal for both. If it is said 'not in samādhi', it is like that for the one who discriminates non-duality.
12.85
विवक्ष्यते तत् अस्माभिः अद्वैतानन्दनामके अध्याये हि तृतीये तत् सर्वम् अपि अतिमङ्गलम्
That is intended to be spoken by us indeed in the third chapter named Advaitānanda. All that is extremely auspicious.
12.86
सदा पश्यन् निजानन्दम् अपश्यन् अखिलम् जगत् अर्थात् योगी इति चेत् तर्हि सन्तुष्टः वर्धताम् भवान्
If [you say] 'Always seeing his own bliss and not seeing the entire world, therefore [he is a] yogin'... then may your honor be satisfied and prosper.
12.87
ब्रह्मानन्दाभिधे ग्रन्थे मन्दानुग्रहसिद्धये द्वितीये अध्याये एतस्मिन् आत्मानन्दः विवेचितः
In this second chapter in the treatise named Brahmānanda, Ātmānanda has been discussed for the accomplishment of grace towards dull students.
