About the book

ब्रह्मसूत्रे प्रपञ्चमिथ्यात्वम् — The falsity of the world (prapañca-mithyātva) in the Brahma-sūtras, by Mahamahopadhyaya Dr. R. Krishnamurthy Sastrigal.

This monograph was compiled from his talk at the vidvat-sadas of the Tiruchirappalli (Trichy) Advaita Sabhā, and published as part of the Sabhā's 46th Annual Report; the sadas was held from 23 to 27 December 1970.

It shows that each of the five classical definitions of mithyātva (falsity) given by the later Advaita teachers is already taught in the Brahma-sūtras, and answers the objection that the world's falsity is Śaṅkara's own invention.

ப்ரம்ஹம் தான் முக்கால ஸத்யம், உலகம் ‘மித்யை’, ஜீவனும் ப்ரம்ஹமும் ஒன்றே, வேறில்லை. இது தான் அத்வைதக் கொள்கையின் ஸாரம். இவற்றுள் ப்ரம்ஹம்தான் உண்மை, ஜீவனும் ப்ரம்ஹமும் வேறில்லை என்பது அனுபவத்திற்கு வரவேண்டுமாகில், உலகம் ‘மித்யை’ என்று புரிந்தால்தான் முடியும். இந்த விஷயத்தை நமக்கு போதிப்பது உபனிஷத்துக்கள் மட்டுமே. ஆனால் உபனிஷத்துக்களை மட்டும் வாசித்து உண்மைகளை தெளிவாக புரிந்துகொள்ள முடியாது என்பதற்காக ஸ்ரீ வேதவ்யாஸர் அவர்களால் ப்ரம்ஹ ஸூத்ரம் என்ற நூல் செய்யப்பட்டது. இந்த நூலிற்கும் பல மதத்தினரும் உரை எழுதியிருக்கின்றனர். அத்வைத மதப் ப்ரசாரத்தில் ப்ரஸித்தரான ஸ்ரீ ஆதிசங்கர பகவத்பாதர் அவர்கள் உரையில் மேற்கூறிய உண்மைகளை தெளிவாக வெளிப்படுத்தியிருக்கிறார்கள். ஆனால் மற்ற மதத்தினர், உபனிஷத்துக்களின் நோக்குக்கு விரிவாக அமைந்த ஆதார நூலான ப்ரம்ஹ ஸூத்ரத்தில் ஸ்ரீ வேதவ்யாஸர் உலகம் ‘மித்யை’ என்பது பற்றி சொல்லவில்லை என்றும், அது ஸ்ரீ சங்கரரின் கற்பனை என்றும் ஆக்ஷேபிக்கிறார்கள். ‘மித்யை’ என்றால் ‘முற்றும் இல்லாதது’ என்று பொருள் கொண்டு கொள்கைகளை புரிந்துகொள்ளாமல் வாதம் செய்கிறார்கள். ப்ரம்ஹத்துடன் ஒன்று கூடியபின் இதில் வாதம் ஏற்படாதுபோனாலும், இம்மார்க்கத்தில் சென்று, குரு, ஈஸ்வர ப்ரஸாதத்தினால், உபனிஷத் மார்க்கத்தால் அத்வைதானந்தத்தை அனுபவிக்க முயற்சி செய்யும் ஆஸ்திகர்களுக்கு உலகம் ‘மித்யை’ என்றால் என்னவென்றும், அது ஆதார நூலான ப்ரம்ஹ ஸூத்ரத்திலேயே பல முறையிலும் விளக்கப்பட்டிருக்கிறது என்பதையும் தெளிவுபடுத்தும் முறையில், இந்த சிறிய நூலை இச்சபை ஆசார்யாள் பாதங்களில் ஸமர்ப்பிக்கின்றது. இதைப் படித்து பயனுறுமாறு கேட்டுக்கொள்கிறோம்.

Brahman alone is real across all three periods of time (trikāla-satya); the world is false (mithyā); the jīva and Brahman are one and the same, not different. This indeed is the essence of the Advaita doctrine. Among these, if the realization that Brahman alone is real and that the jīva and Brahman are non-different is to become a matter of direct experience (anubhava), it is possible only if one understands that the world is mithyā. It is the Upaniṣads alone that teach us this matter. However, so that the truths may be understood clearly—since that cannot be achieved merely by reading the Upaniṣads alone—the work called the Brahma-sūtra was composed by Śrī Veda-vyāsa. Commentaries on this work have been written by adherents of various traditions. Śrī Ādi Śaṅkara Bhagavatpāda, renowned for propagating the Advaita tradition, has clearly brought to light the aforesaid truths in his commentary. But proponents of other traditions object that Śrī Veda-vyāsa has not spoken of the world being mithyā in the Brahma-sūtra, which is the foundational text elaborating the intent of the Upaniṣads, and that this is merely Śrī Śaṅkara's imagination. Taking the word mithyā to mean 'completely non-existent', they argue without understanding the tenets. Although no debate on this can arise after attaining oneness with Brahman, for faithful seekers (āstikas) who proceed along this path and endeavor to experience the bliss of Advaita (advaitānanda) through the path of the Upaniṣads by the grace of Guru and Īśvara, this Sabhā dedicates this small treatise at the feet of the Ācārya, clarifying what is meant by the world being mithyā and how it has been explained in various ways in the foundational text, the Brahma-sūtra itself. We request [readers] to read this and be benefited.

(S. R. கிருஷ்ணமூர்த்தி சாஸ்திரீ)

(S. R. Krishnamurthy Shastri)

ஸ்ரீ பரமேஸ்வரன் ஸ்ரீமத் ஆதிசங்கர பகவத்பாத ரூபமாக அவதரித்து அத்வைத தர்சனம் என்கிற வேதாந்த தர்சனத்தை உலகம் முழுவதும் ப்ரசாரம் செய்வதற்கு முன்பே, அநாதியாக பல மஹரிஷிகள் அத்வைத தர்சனத்தையே (கொள்கை) தான் ஔபநிஷத தர்சனம் என்று தங்கள் க்ரந்தங்களில் குறிப்பிட்டிருக்கிறார்கள். பிறகும், பல ஆஸ்திக நாஸ்திக க்ரந்தங்களில், விசேஷமாக ஜைன பௌத்த க்ரந்தங்களில் அத்வைத தர்சனத்தையே ஒளபநிஷத தர்சனம் என்று குறிப்பிடுகிறார்கள். உபநிஷத்துகளை (வேதாந்தங்களை) ப்ரமாணமாகக் கொண்டது அத்வைத தர்சனம் என்பது இதனால் ஸ்பஷ்டமாகிறது. உபநிஷத்துகளையே ப்ரமாணமாகக்கொண்டு ஸ்ரீ பகவத் பாதரும் அத்வைத தர்சனத்தை விளக்கியிருக்கிறார்கள். இவ்வுபநிஷத்துக்களில் உள்ள வாக்யங்களின் தாத்பர்யங்களை, ப்ருஹ்ம ஸூத்ரத்தில் ஸ்ரீவேத வ்யாஸர், பல நியாயங்களின் மூலமாக நிர்ணயம் செய்திருக்கிறார்கள்.

Even before Śrī Parameśvara incarnated in the form of Śrīmat Ādi Śaṅkara Bhagavatpāda to propagate the Vedāntic philosophy known as Advaita Darśana throughout the world, many great sages (maharṣis) from beginningless time have designated Advaita Darśana (doctrine) alone as the Aupaniṣada Darśana (the philosophy of the Upaniṣads) in their texts. Subsequently as well, in many orthodox (āstika) and heterodox (nāstika) texts, especially in Jaina and Buddhist works, they refer to Advaita Darśana alone as the Aupaniṣada Darśana. From this it becomes evident that Advaita Darśana is that which has the Upaniṣads (Vedāntas) as its means of valid knowledge (pramāṇa). Taking the Upaniṣads alone as pramāṇa, Śrī Bhagavatpāda too elucidated the Advaita Darśana. In the Brahma-sūtra, Śrī Veda-vyāsa has determined the purport (tātparya) of the statements in these Upaniṣads by means of various logical arguments (nyāyas).

அத்வைதம் என்றால் ஸத்யவஸ்து ஒன்றே என்பது தான். ப்ரத்யக்ஷாதி ப்ரமாணங்களினால் பல வஸ்துக்கள் அறியப்படுகின்றனவே என்று ஸந்தேஹம் வரலாம். இதற்கு அத்வைத ஸித்தாந்தத்தில் சொல்லும் சமாதானமாவது: - ஸ்வயம் பிரகாச பூர்ணானந்த ரூபமான ஆத்ம ஸ்வரூபம் ஒன்றுதான் ஸத்யம். இந்த ஒன்றே பலவாகத் தோன்றுகிறது. உண்மையில் ஒன்றாக இருந்து பலவாகத் தோன்றும் காரணத்தினால்தான் இதற்கு प्रपञ्चம் என்ற பெயர் அமைந்திருக்கிறது. உண்மையில் பலது இருக்குமானால் அதற்கு प्रपञ्चம் என்ற பெயர் பொருத்தமுடையதாக இருக்காது. प्रपञ्च என்றால் விஸ்தாரம். ஒன்றே விஸ்தாரம் அடைகிறது என்பதுதான் பொருத்தம். பலது இருப்பதை விஸ்தாரமாயிருப்பது என்று சொல்வதில்லை. ஆக, தெரிகிற வஸ்துக்கள் (दृश्यम्) தெரிகின்ற காரணத்தினால், ஸத்யமாக இருக்க வேண்டுமென்பது இல்லை. கயிறு ஸர்ப்பமாகத் தெரியும் 4காரணத்தினால் ஸர்ப்பம் ஸத்யமாகாது. ஆக ஸத்யம் ஒன்றே என்ற அத்வைத ஸித்தாந்தத்திற்கு ப்ரத்யக்ஷாதி ப்ரமாணங்களினால் ஸித்தமான லோக வ்யவஹாரத்தினாலும் ஒருவித ஹானியும் இல்லை.

Advaita means that the real entity (satya-vastu) is one alone. A doubt may arise: 'Are not many entities known through means of knowledge such as perception (pratyakṣa)?' To this, the resolution offered in the Advaita Siddhānta is as follows: The nature of the Self (ātma-svarūpa), which is self-luminous (svayaṃ-prakāśa) and of the nature of plenary bliss (pūrṇānanda-rūpa), is the one reality. This one alone appears as many. It is precisely because it is in reality one and appears as manifold that the name prapañca (world/diversification) has been applied to it. If in reality there were many things, the name prapañca would not be appropriate for it. Prapañca means expansion or manifold appearance (vistāra). It is proper to say that the one alone undergoes expansion. The presence of multiple things is not called 'being an expansion'. Therefore, perceived objects (dṛśyam), merely because they are perceived, need not be real. Just because a rope is perceived as a snake, the snake does not become real. Thus, the Advaita Siddhānta that reality is one alone suffers no damage whatsoever from worldly transactions (loka-vyavahāra) established through means of knowledge such as perception.

இவ்வாறாகில், தோன்றுகிற ப்ரபஞ்சத்தின் ஸ்வரூபம் என்ன என்று கேட்போமாகில், தோன்றுகிற ப்ரபஞ்சம் ஸத்யமல்ல, மித்யை என்று வேதாந்தங்கள் கூறுகின்றன.

If that is so, if it is asked what the nature (svarūpa) of the appearing world (prapañca) is, the Vedāntas declare that the appearing world is not real (satya), but false (mithyā).

ப்ரபஞ்சத்தின் மித்யாத்வத்தை ப்ராசீன க்ரந்தங்களில் கொடுத்திருக்கிற முக்கியமான ஐந்து விளக்கங்களின் மூலமாக விளக்கி, இவ்வித ப்ரபஞ்ச மித்யாத்வம் எவ்வாறு ப்ருஹ்ம ஸூத்ரங்களில் குறிப்பிடப்படுகின்றன என்பதைக் கீழே காண்போம்.

Explaining the falsity (mithyātva) of the world through the five major definitions given in ancient texts, we shall see below how such world-falsity is indicated in the Brahma-sūtras.

அந்த ஐந்து மித்யாத்வத்தின் லக்ஷணங்களாவன: (1) सदसद्विलक्षणत्वं; உள்ளது அல்லது இல்லாதது என்று நிச்சயிக்க முடியாதது; (2) प्रतिपन्नोपाधौ त्रैकालिकनिषेधप्रतियोगित्वं ஒன்று இருப்பதாக தெரிகின்ற இடத்திலேயே அது உண்மையில் இல்லாமல் இருப்பது; (3) ज्ञाननिवर्त्यत्वं, ஞானத்தினால் இல்லாமல் போவது; (4) स्वात्यन्ताभावाधिकरणे एव प्रतीयमानत्वं உண்மையில் தான் இல்லாத இடத்திலேயே இருப்பதாக தோன்றுவது; (5) सद्भिन्नत्वं பரமார்த்தஸத்தாய் இல்லாதது.

Those five definitions (lakṣaṇas) of mithyātva are: (1) sadasadvilakṣaṇatvam—being distinct from the real (sat) and the unreal (asat), i.e., that which cannot be determined as either existent or non-existent; (2) pratipannopādhau traikālika-niṣedha-pratiyogitvam—being the counter-positive of a negation across all three times in the very locus where it is perceived, i.e., not existing in reality in the very place where it is perceived to exist; (3) jñāna-nivartyatvam—sublatability by knowledge, i.e., ceasing to be through knowledge; (4) svātyantābhāvādhikaraṇa eva pratīyamānatvam—being perceived only in the locus of its own absolute non-existence, i.e., appearing to exist in the very place where it is in reality absent; (5) sad-bhinnatvam—being different from the real (sat), i.e., not being ultimately real (paramārtha-sat).

IDefinition 1: neither real nor unreal

I. सदसद्विलक्षणत्वं मिथ्यात्वम्।

I. Sadasadvilakṣaṇatvaṃ mithyātvam (Falsity is distinctness from the real and the unreal).

ப்ருஹ்ம ஸூத்ர அத்யாஸ பாஷ்ய வ்யாக்யானத்தில் ஸ்ரீ பத்மபாதர் கூறியிருப்பதாவது: मिथ्याशब्दो द्व्यर्थः— अपह्नव वचनः अनिर्वचनीयतावचनश्च (पञ्चपादिका). இதன்படி ப்ரபஞ்சம் மித்யை என்றால் ப்ரபஞ்சம் இல்லை (சூன்யம்) என்று அர்த்தமல்ல; ப்ரபஞ்சம் ‘அநிர்வசனீயம்’ என்று அர்த்தம். ஆகவே, பஞ்சபாதிகாவில் கூறுகிறபடி, மித்யாத்வம் என்பது அநிர்வசனீயத்வம். (अनिर्वचनीयत्वम्) ஸ்ரீமதுசூதன ஸரஸ்வதி ஸ்வாமிகள் அத்வைத ஸித்தி (अद्वैतसिद्धि) யிலும் ஸ்ரீ பகவத்பாதாள் तदनन्यत्वाधिकरण பாஷ்யத்திலும் 5ஆதலால் ஸத்யம் ஒன்றே என்னும் அத்வைத ஸித்தாந்தத்திற்கு ஒன்றோடும் விரோதமில்லை. अनिर्वचनीयम् என்றால் निर्वचनम् செய்ய முடியாதது, குறிப்பிட்டு சொல்ல முடியாதது என்று அர்த்தமல்ல; सत् என்றோ, असत् என்றோ, सदसत् என்றோ கூறமுடியாதது என்றுதான் அர்த்தம். (सद्भिन्नं, असद्भिन्नं, सदसद्भिन्नं च यत् तत् अनिर्वाच्यम्) இவ்விதம் பழைய அத்வைத க்ரந்தங்களில் கூறியிருப்பதை கவனிக்காமலேயே இதர மதத்தினர்கள் “ப்ரபஞ்சத்தை அத்வைதிகள், மாத்யமிக பௌத்தர்களைப்போல், சூன்யம் என்று சொல்கிறார்கள்” என்றும், “ப்ரபஞ்சத்தை குறிப்பிட்டு சொல்ல முடியாதது என்று சொல்கிறார்கள்” என்றும் தூஷணம் சொல்கிறார்கள். இந்த தூஷணம் சரியல்ல என்பது மேற்கூறியவைகளிலிருந்து விளங்கும்.

In the commentary on the Adhyāsa-bhāṣya of the Brahma-sūtra, Śrī Padmapāda has stated: 'The word mithyā has two meanings—it denotes denial/concealment (apahnava-vacana), and it denotes indeterminability (anirvacanīyatā-vacana)' (Pañcapādikā). According to this, when it is said that the world is mithyā, it does not mean that the world does not exist (is void / śūnya); it means that the world is indeterminable (anirvacanīya). Therefore, as stated in the Pañcapādikā, falsity is indeterminability (anirvacanīyatvam). In the Advaita-siddhi of Śrī Madhusūdana Sarasvatī Svāmin as well as in Śrī Bhagavatpāda's commentary on the Tadananyatvādhikaraṇa, [it is shown that] therefore there is no contradiction whatsoever with the Advaita Siddhānta that reality is one. Anirvacanīyam does not mean that which cannot be defined (nirvacana) or cannot be explicitly stated; it only means that which cannot be categorized either as sat (real), asat (unreal), or sadasat (both real and unreal) (sad-bhinnaṃ, asad-bhinnaṃ, sadasad-bhinnaṃ ca yat tat anirvācyam). Without noticing what has been thus stated in ancient Advaita works, adherents of other schools criticize Advaitins, saying, 'Advaitins declare the world to be void (śūnya), like the Mādhyamika Buddhists,' and 'They say the world cannot be spoken of.' That this criticism is invalid will be clear from what has been said above.

“नासदासीत् नो सदासीत्.........तम आसीत्” என்ற ச்ருதி வாக்யம் இந்த மித்யாத்வத்தைக் கூறுகிறது. இந்த வாக்யத்தில் ப்ரளயத்தில் ஸத் இல்லை, அஸத் இல்லை, தமஸ் (तमस् = ப்ரபஞ்சத்தினுடைய ப்ரக்ருதி, அவித்யா, மாயா) இருந்தது என்று சொல்லப்பட்டிருக்கிறது. இதனால் ப்ரபஞ்சத்தினுடைய ப்ரக்ருதி ஸத் அல்ல, அஸத் அல்ல, அநிர்வசனீயம் என்று கூறப்பட்டதாக ஆகிறது. ப்ரபஞ்சத்தினுடைய காரணமான ப்ரக்ருதி அநிர்வசனீயமாக இருக்க, அதன் கார்யமான ப்ரபஞ்சம் அநிர்வசனீயம் என்பது தனிப்பட்டு கூறாமலே விளங்கும். இந்த அர்த்தம் तदनन्यत्वाधिकरण (ப்ரம்ஹஸூத்ர) பாஷ்யத்திலும் ஸ்பஷ்டமாக்கப்பட்டிருக்கிறது.

The śruti passage 'There was neither the non-existent (nāsadāsīt) nor the existent (no sadāsīt)... there was darkness (tama āsīt)' (Ṛgveda 10.129.1, 3) declares this falsity (mithyātva). In this passage, it is said that in dissolution (pralaya) there was no sat, no asat, but darkness (tamas = the primordial material cause / prakṛti of the world, avidyā, māyā) was present. By this, it amounts to saying that the material cause of the world is neither sat nor asat, but anirvacanīya. When prakṛti, the cause of the world, is anirvacanīya, it is self-evident without needing to be stated separately that its effect, the world, is also anirvacanīya. This meaning is made clear in the commentary on the Tadananyatvādhikaraṇa (of the Brahma-sūtra) as well:

ब्रह्मसूत्रभाष्यम् २-१-१४सर्वज्ञस्येश्वरस्यात्मभूते इवाविद्याकल्पिते नामरूपे तत्वान्यत्वाभ्यामनिर्वचनीये संसारप्रपञ्चबीजभूते सर्वज्ञस्येश्वरस्य मायाशक्तिः प्रकृतिरिति च श्रुतिस्मृत्योरभिलप्येते। (ब्र. सू. भा. २-१-१४)

'Name and form (nāmarūpe), imagined through ignorance (avidyā-kalpite), being, as it were, identical with the omniscient Lord (sarvajñasyeśvarasyātmabhūte iva), indeterminable either as that reality or as different from it (tattvānyatvābhyām anirvacanīye), the seed of the transmigratory universe (saṃsāra-prapañca-bīja-bhūte), are spoken of in śruti and smṛti as the power of illusion (māyā-śaktiḥ) and the primordial nature (prakṛtiḥ) of the omniscient Lord.' (BS bhāṣya 2.1.14)

सदसद्विलक्षणत्वम् என்ற இந்த மித்யாத்வம் ப்ரபஞ்சத்திற்கு பிருஹ்ம ஸூத்ரங்களில் நிர்ணயிக்கப்பட்டிருக்கிறது. तथाहि: सत् என்பது முக்காலத்திலும் பாதிக்கப்படாதது, (त्रिकालाबाध्यं), உண்மையாக உள்ளது, ஒரு காலத்திலும் 6இல்லாமற் போவது இல்லை. இந்த வஸ்து அத்வைத ஸித்தாந்தத்தின்படி ஆத்ம ஸ்வரூபம் தான். असत् என்பது ஒரு காலத்திலும் இல்லாதது, சூன்யம், ஸத்தாகவும், அஸத்தாகவும் இருப்பது ஸதஸத் (सदसत्). ப்ரபஞ்சம் இந்த மூன்று விதமும் அல்ல, நான்காவதான अनिर्वचनीयम्.

This falsity in the form of distinctness from the real and the unreal (sadasadvilakṣaṇatvam) is determined for the world in the Brahma-sūtras. To explain (tathāhi): Sat (the real) is that which is uncontradicted in all three times (trikālābādhya), truly existing, and never ceases to exist at any time. According to Advaita Siddhānta, this entity is indeed the nature of the Self (ātma-svarūpa). Asat (the unreal) is that which does not exist at any time, an absolute void (śūnya); and that which is both real and unreal is sadasat. The world is not of these three kinds, but is the fourth, anirvacanīya (indeterminable).

ப்ரபஞ்சம் सत् அல்ல என்பது असम्भवस्तु सतोऽनुपपत्तेः (ब्र.सू - 2-3-9) என்ற ப்ரம்ஹ ஸூத்ரத்தினால் அறிவிக்கப்படுகிறது.

That the world is not sat (real/existent) is made known by the Brahma-sūtra: "There is, however, no origination of the Existent (sat), because of impossibility" (asambhavastu sato'nupapatteḥ, BS 2.3.9).

இந்த ஸூத்ரத்தில் सत् வஸ்துவுக்கு உற்பத்தியில்லை என்று சொல்லப்பட்டிருக்கிறது. ப்ரபஞ்சத்திற்கோ உபநிஷத்துகளில் உற்பத்தி சொல்லப்பட்டிருக்கிறது. ப்ருஹ்ம ஸூத்ரத்திலும் 2 வது அத்யாயம் 3, 4 பாதங்களில் ப்ரபஞ்சத்திற்கு உற்பத்தியுண்டு என்று நிச்சயிக்கப்பட்டிருக்கிறது. ஸத்வஸ்துவுக்கு உற்பத்தி கிடையாது என்றும், ப்ரபஞ்சத்திற்கு உற்பத்தியுண்டு என்றும் உபபாதனம் செய்யும் ஸூத்ரங்கள் ப்ரபஞ்சம் ஸத்தல்ல என்பதை ஸ்பஷ்டமாக்குகின்றன.

In this sūtra, it is stated that there is no origination (utpatti) for the entity called sat. For the world, on the other hand, origination is spoken of in the Upaniṣads. In the Brahma-sūtra as well, in the third and fourth pādas of the second adhyāya, it is established that the world has origination. The sūtras that establish that the entity sat has no origination, while the world does have origination, make it clear that the world is not sat.

यावद्विकारन्तु विभागो लोकवत् (ब्र - सू - 2-3-7) என்ற ப்ரம்ஹ ஸூத்ரத்தினால் வெவ்வேறாக தெரிகிற வஸ்துக்களெல்லாம் விகாரம் என்று கூறப்பட்டிருக்கிறது. ப்ரபஞ்சத்திலுள்ள வஸ்துக்களெல்லாம் வெவ்வேறாகத் தோன்றுகின்றன. ஆகையால் அவைகள் விகாரங்கள். விகாரங்கள் எல்லாம் மித்யை என்பது तदनन्यत्वम् आरम्भणशब्दादिभ्यः (2-1-14) என்னும் ப்ரம்ஹ ஸூத்ரத்தினால் தெரிகிறது. இந்த ஸூத்ரம் ஜகத் காரண விசார ப்ரகரணத்தில் இருப்பதால் तत् என்பதற்கு ஜகத் காரணம் என்று அர்த்தம். तदनन्यत्वम् என்பதற்கு ப்ரபஞ்சம், காரண வஸ்துவை விட்டு உண்மையில் வேறில்லை என்று அர்த்தம். இதற்கு உபபத்தியாக आरम्भणशब्दादिभ्यः என்று ஸூத்ரத்தில் கூறப்பட்டிருக்கிறது. आरम्भणशब्द என்பதினால் वाचारम्भणम् विकारो नामधेयम् मृत्तिकेत्येव सत्यम् (छा ६-१-४) என்ற ச்ருதி வாக்யம் எடுத்துக்காட்டப்படுகிறது. இந்த ச்ருதி வாக்யத்தில் ம்ருத்திகை, அதன் விகாரமான घटादि களை எடுத்துக்கொண்டு ம்ருத்திகை (காரணம்) தான் 7ஸத்யம் என்று சொல்லப்பட்டிருக்கிறது. अर्थात् விகாரங்களெல்லாம் (घटादि) மித்யை என்று விளங்குகிறது.

By the Brahma-sūtra, "Division, however, extends as far as modification, as in the world" (yāvad-vikāraṃ tu vibhāgo lokavat, BS 2.3.7), it is stated that all things that appear differentiated are modifications (vikāra). All entities in the world appear differentiated. Therefore, they are modifications. That all modifications are mithyā (false/illusory) is understood from the Brahma-sūtra, "Non-otherness [of cause and effect], on account of words such as ārambhaṇa (taking hold / originating), etc." (tad-ananyatvam ārambhaṇa-śabdādibhyaḥ, BS 2.1.14). Since this sūtra occurs in the context of inquiring into the cause of the world (jagat-kāraṇa-vicāra-prakaraṇa), tat ("that") means the cause of the world. Tadananyatvam ("their non-otherness") means that the world is in reality not distinct from the causal entity. As the reasoning for this, it is stated in the sūtra, ārambhaṇa-śabdādibhyaḥ ("from words such as ārambhaṇa, etc."). By the expression ārambhaṇa-śabda, the śruti text, "A modification is a grasping by speech, a mere name; the clay alone is real" (vācārambhaṇaṃ vikāro nāmadheyaṃ mṛttikety eva satyam, Ch. Up. 6.1.4), is cited. In this śruti text, taking clay and its modifications such as pots, etc. (ghaṭādi), it is stated that clay (the cause) alone is real (satyam). That is (arthāt), it is understood that all modifications (pots, etc.) are mithyā.

विकारमिथ्यात्वத்தைக் கூறுகிற ச்ருதி வாக்யத்தைக் கொண்டு இந்த ஸூத்ரம் तदनन्यत्वं என்று கூறுவதால் விகாரமெல்லாம் காரண ஸ்வரூபத்தைக் காட்டிலும் உண்மையான தனிப்பட்ட ஸ்வரூபமற்றது. (कारणसत्ताऽतिरिक्तसत्ताकत्वाभावः), காரணம்தான் ஸத், விகாரம் ஸத்தல்ல என்று விளக்குகிறது. ஆகவே यावद्विकारन्तु என்ற ब्रह्म ஸூத்ரத்தினால் வெவ்வேறாகத் தெரிகின்ற ப்ரபஞ்சமெல்லாம் விகாரம் என்றும், तदनन्यत्वम् என்ற ஸூத்ரத்தினால் விகாரமெல்லாம் காரண ஸத்தையைக் காட்டிலும் வேறு ஸத்தையற்றது என்றும் கூறப்பட்டபடியால் விகாரமான ப்ரபஞ்சம் ஸத்தல்ல என்பது தெளிவாகிறது.

Since this sūtra, relying on the śruti passage that speaks of the falsity of modifications (vikāra-mithyātva), states tadananyatvam (non-otherness from that), it explains that all modifications lack a real independent existence distinct from the nature of the cause (kāraṇa-sattātiriktasattākatvābhāvaḥ); the cause alone is sat, and the modification is not sat. Therefore, since by the Brahma-sūtra yāvadvikārantu... it is said that the entire world perceived as distinct is a modification, and by the sūtra tadananyatvam... it is said that all modifications have no existence other than the existence of the cause, it is clear that the world, being a modification, is not sat.

सत्वाच्चावरस्य (2–1–16) என்ற ஸூத்ரமும் एकं च पुनः सत्वं என்ற பாஷ்யமும், கார்யத்தில் सत्वं இல்லை காரணத்தில் இருக்கும் सत्वं தான் கார்யத்தில் இருப்பதாகத் தோன்றுகிறது என்பதையும் விளக்குகிறது.

The sūtra 'And because of the existence of the posterior [effect in the cause]' (sattvāccāvarasya, BS 2.1.16) and the bhāṣya [statement] 'And again, existence is one' (ekaṃ ca punaḥ sattvam) also explain that existence (sattva) does not belong to the effect; rather, the existence present in the cause alone appears as though belonging to the effect.

இவ்விதம் ப்ரபஞ்சம் ஸத்தல்ல என்பதை ஸூத்ரத்தினால் விளக்கினோம்.

In this manner, we have explained through the sūtra that the world is not sat (real).

ப்ரபஞ்சம் असत् (शून्यम्) அல்ல என்பதும் ஸூத்ரத்தினால் விளங்குகிறது.

That the world is not asat (void / śūnya) also becomes evident through the sūtra.

नाभावः उपलब्धेः (2-2-28) என்ற ब्रह्म ஸூத்ரம் ப்ரபஞ்சம் (दृश्यं) சூன்யம் என்று கூறுகிற பௌத்த (விஞ்ஞான வாதி) மதத்தை நிராகரணம் செய்கிறது. दृश्य மான ப்ரபஞ்சம் दृश्यமாக இருக்கும் காரணத்தினாலேயே அஸத் அல்ல, சூன்யம் அல்ல என்று ஸூத்ரத்தில் கூறப்படுகிறது.

The Brahma-sūtra "There is no non-existence [of external objects], because they are perceived" (nābhāva upalabdheḥ, BS 2.2.28) refutes the Buddhist (Vijñānavādin) school which asserts that the world, the object of perception (dṛśya), is void (śūnya). It is stated in the sūtra that precisely because the perceived world is an object of perception (dṛśya), it is not non-existent (asat), nor is it void (śūnya).

ஸத்வம், அஸத்வம் என்ற விருத்த தர்மங்களின் சேர்க்கையையும் ஸூத்ரங்கள் நிராகரிக்கின்றன. नैकस्मिन्नसम्भवात् என்ற ஸூத்ரம் एवं चात्माकार्त्स्न्यम् (2-2-34) என்ற அடுத்த ஸூத்ரத்தை அனுசரித்து பார்க்குங்கால், स्यादस्ति स्यान्नास्ति என்று விருத்த தர்மங்களின் சேர்க்கையைச் சொல்லும் ஜைன 8மதத்தை நிராகரிப்பதாகத் தெரிகிறது. न ह्येकस्मिन् धर्मिणि युगपत् सदसत्वादिविरुद्धधर्मसमावेशः सम्भवति शीतोष्णवत् என்ற பாஷ்ய வாக்யமும் விருத்த தர்மங்களின் சேர்க்கை அஸம்பாவிதம் என்பதை தெளிவாக்குகிறது. இதனால் ப்ரபஞ்சம் सदसत् ரூபமல்ல என்பது விளங்குகிறது.

The sūtras also refute the coexistence of contradictory attributes such as existence (satva) and non-existence (asatva). When the sūtra "Not in one [locus], on account of impossibility" (naikasminnasambhavāt, BS 2.2.33) is considered in conjunction with the subsequent sūtra "And likewise, there would be non-entirety of the Self" (evaṁ cātmākārtsnyam, BS 2.2.34), it is seen to refute the Jaina school, which asserts the coexistence of contradictory attributes through "maybe it is, maybe it is not" (syādasti syānnāsti). The bhāṣya statement, "Indeed, the simultaneous coexistence of contradictory properties such as existence and non-existence in a single substratum is impossible, like cold and heat" (na hyekasmin dharmiṇi yugapat sadasatvādi-viruddhadharma-samāveśaḥ sambhavati śītoṣṇavat), also makes it clear that the coexistence of contradictory properties is impossible. From this, it is clear that the world is not of the nature of being both real and unreal (sadasat).

இவ்விதம், सद्भिन्नं, असद्भिन्नं, सदसद्भिन्नं च यत् तत् अनिर्वच्यं, मिथ्या என்று பஞ்சபாதிகாவில் கூறப்படுகிற மித்யாத்வம் ப்ரபஞ்சத்திற்கு ஸூத்ரங்களிலும் குறிப்பிடப்பட்டிருக்கிறது.

Thus, the definition of falsity stated in the Pañcapādikā—"that which is distinct from the real (sad-bhinna), distinct from the unreal (asad-bhinna), and distinct from both real and unreal (sadasad-bhinna) is indeterminable (anirvācya), false (mithyā)"—is also indicated for the world in the sūtras.

IIDefinition 2: negated, in all three times, in the very place it appears

II. प्रतिपन्नोपाधौ त्रैकालिकनिषेधप्रतियोगित्वं मिथ्यात्वम्।

II. Falsity is being the counterpositive of a negation across all three divisions of time in the very locus where it is perceived (pratipannopādhau traikālika-niṣedha-pratiyogitvaṁ mithyātvam).

தான் இருப்பதாகத் தெரிகின்ற இடத்திலேயே, உண்மையில் அது இல்லை என்பது மித்யாத்வம் என்று पञ्चपादिका विवरणத்தில் மித்யாத்வம் விளக்கப்படுகிறது.

In the Pañcapādikā-vivaraṇa, falsity (mithyātva) is explained as: not existing in reality in the very locus where it appears to exist.

ब्रह्मसूत्रम् ३-२-२२प्रकृतैतावत्वं हि प्रतिषेधति ततो ब्रवीति च भूयः (3-2-22)

"For [the text] denies the previously mentioned limited nature [of Brahman], and then speaks further" (prakṛtaitāvattvaṁ hi pratiṣedhati tato bravīti ca bhūyaḥ, BS 3.2.22).

என்ற ब्रह्म ஸூத்ரத்தினால் ப்ரபஞ்சத்திற்கு இந்த மித்யாத்வம் விளக்கப்படுகிறது. अथात आदेशो नेति नेति (बृ. २-३-६) என்ற ச்ருதி வாக்யம் இந்த ஸூத்ரத்தில் விசாரிக்கப்படுகிறது. द्वे वाव ब्रह्मणो रूपे என்ற முன் ச்ருதி வாக்யத்தில் ப்ருஹ்மத்திற்கு ப்ரபஞ்சரூபம் சொல்லப்பட்டிருக்கிறது. नेति नेति என்ற வாக்யத்தில் இந்த ரூபம் நிஷேதிக்கப்படுகிறது. ப்ருஹ்மத்தில் ப்ரபஞ்சம் இருப்பதாக அறிவித்து, பிறகு ப்ருஹ்மத்தில் ப்ரபஞ்சம் இல்லை என்று ச்ருதி கூறுகிறபடியால் ப்ரபஞ்சம் தான் தோன்றுகிற ப்ருஹ்மத்தில் உண்மையாக இல்லை என்பது தெளிவாகிறது. இந்த ச்ருதி வாக்யத்திற்கு இது தான் அர்த்தம் என்பது இந்த ஸூத்ரத்தினால் தெரிவிக்கப்படுகிறது. नेति नेति என்ற வாக்யத்தில் காலத்தைக் குறிப்பிடாததால், ப்ரபஞ்சம் முக்காலத்திலும் உண்மையாக இல்லை என்பதும் தெளிவாகிறது. द्वे वाव ब्रह्मणो रूपे என்று ச்ருதி ப்ருஹ்மத்திற்கு ப்ரபஞ்ச ரூபத்தை ஒத்துக்கொண்டிருக்கிறபடியால், ப்ரபஞ்சம் சூன்யம் (அஸத்) அல்ல என்பதும் ஸ்பஷ்டமாகிறது. ஆகவே, இந்த ஸூத்ரத்தில் प्रतिपन्नोपाधौ त्रैकालिकनिषेधप्रतियोगित्वम् என்கிற மித்யாத்வம் கூறப்பட்டதாக ஆகிறது.

By this Brahma-sūtra, this definition of falsity is explained with regard to the world. The śruti passage "Now therefore the teaching: 'Not this, not this'" (athāta ādeśo neti neti, Bṛ. Up. 2.3.6) is examined in this sūtra. In the earlier śruti passage, "There are indeed two forms of Brahman" (dve vāva brahmaṇo rūpe, Bṛ. Up. 2.3.1), the forms of the world are attributed to Brahman. In the passage "Not this, not this" (neti neti), these forms are negated. Since the śruti first indicates the presence of the world in Brahman and then declares that the world is not in Brahman, it is clear that the world does not truly exist in Brahman, which is its substratum of appearance. That this is the meaning of this śruti passage is conveyed by this sūtra. Since no specific time is mentioned in the statement neti neti, it is also evident that the world is not real across all three periods of time (past, present, future). Furthermore, because the śruti has acknowledged the cosmic forms in Brahman by saying dve vāva brahmaṇo rūpe, it is clear that the world is not void (śūnya / asat). Therefore, this sūtra establishes falsity in the sense of being the counterpositive of a negation across all three divisions of time in the very locus where it is perceived (pratipannopādhau traikālika-niṣedha-pratiyogitvam).

IIIDefinition 3: removable by knowledge

9III. ज्ञाननिवर्त्यत्वं मिथ्यात्वम्।

III. Falsity is sublatability by knowledge (jñāna-nivartyatvaṁ mithyātvam).

ஞானத்தினால் எது மறைகிறதோ அது மித்யை என்ற ज्ञाननिवर्त्यत्वம் என்ற மித்யாத்வமும் पञ्चपादिकाविवरणத்தில் விளக்கப்பட்டிருக்கிறது.

The definition of falsity (mithyātva) as sublatability by knowledge (jñāna-nivartyatva)—that whatever is terminated by knowledge is false—is also elucidated in the Pañcapādikā-vivaraṇa.

प्रपञ्चத்திற்கு இந்த மித்யாத்வம் अथातो ब्रह्मजिज्ञासा (ब्र. सू. 1-1-1) என்ற ब्रह्म ஸூத்ரத்தில் தெரிவிக்கப்படுகிறது. மோக்ஷத்தை விரும்புகிறவருக்கு இந்த ஸூத்ரம் பிருஹ்மஞானத்தை உபாயமாக கூறுகிறது. மோக்ஷம் என்பது பந்த நிவ்ருத்தி (बन्धनिवृत्ति). ஜன்மம், மரணம் இவைகளுக்கிடையே உள்ள வ்யவஹாரங்கள் பந்தமாகக் கருதப்படுகின்றன. இவைகளுக்குக் காரணம் புண்யபாப கர்மாக்கள். कर्तृत्वभोक्तृत्व மேஜீவனுடைய பந்தம் என்று சுருக்கமாகக் கூறலாம். இந்த பந்தம் பிருஹ்ம ஞானத்தினால் நிவ்ருத்தியடைகிறது என்று மோக்ஷஸாதனமாகப் ப்ரஹ்மஞ்ஞானத்தைக் கூறுகின்ற இந்த ஸூத்ரத்தினால் தெரிகிறது. ஞானத்தினால் நிவர்த்தியடைவது அஞானம் (अज्ञानम्) தான் என்பது எல்லோரும் ஒத்துக்கொள்ளக் கூடியது. பந்தம் ஞானத்தினால் நிவர்த்தியடைகிறது என்று ஸூத்ரத்தில் கூறியிருக்கிறபடியால் பந்தம் அஞானகார்யம் (अज्ञानकार्य) என்பது நிச்சயமாகிறது. ரஜ்ஜுசர்ப்பம் (रज्जुसर्प) முதலிய அஞான கார்யமெல்லாம் ஸத்யமல்ல என்பது தெரிகிறது. இதனால் பிருஹ்மஞானத்தினால், நிவ்ருத்தியடையக்கூடிய கர்த்ருத்வ, போக்த்ருத்வ ரூபமான ஜீவனுடைய பந்தம் ஸத்யமல்ல, மித்யை என்று ஏற்படுகிறது. போக்த்ருத்வம் மித்யையானால் போக்யமான ப்ரபஞ்சமும் மித்யையாகத்தான் இருக்க வேண்டும். தான் ராஜ்யாபிஷேகம் செய்து கொண்டதாக ஒருவன் கண்ட ஸ்வப்னத்தில் அவனுடைய ராஜ பரிவாரங்கள் முதலியன ஸத்யமாக இருக்க முடியாது. ஆகையால், ப்ரும்ஹ ஞானத்தினால் போக்த்ருத்வம் நிவ்ருத்தியடையும்போது, போக்யப்ரபஞ்சத்திற்கும் நிவ்ருத்தி ஏற்படுகிறது. ஜடப் பிரபஞ்சம் முழுதும் போக்யமே. ஞானத்தினால் இதனுடைய நிவ்ருத்தி ஏற்படுவதாக ஸூத்ரம் கூறுவதால், ப்ரபஞ்சம் அஞான கார்யம், ஸத்யமல்ல, மித்யை என்று ஸூத்ரத்தில் கூறப்பட்டதாக ஆகிறது.

For the world, this definition of falsity is conveyed in the Brahma-sūtra "Now, therefore, the inquiry into Brahman" (athāto brahma-jijñāsā, BS 1.1.1). This sūtra prescribes the knowledge of Brahman as the means for one who desires liberation (mokṣa). Liberation is the cessation of bondage (bandha-nivṛtti). The transmigratory dealings between birth and death are considered bondage. The cause for these is the actions of merit and demerit (puṇya-pāpa-karma). In brief, agency and enjoyership (kartṛtva-bhoktṛtva) constitute the jīva's bondage. That this bondage is terminated by the knowledge of Brahman is known from this sūtra, which declares knowledge of Brahman to be the means to liberation. It is universally accepted that only ignorance (ajñāna) is terminated by knowledge. Since the sūtra states that bondage is terminated by knowledge, it is certain that bondage is an effect of ignorance (ajñāna-kārya). It is well known that all effects of ignorance, such as the rope-snake (rajju-sarpa), are not real. Consequently, the bondage of the jīva, consisting of agency and enjoyership that can be terminated by Brahman-knowledge, is not real but false (mithyā). If enjoyership is false, the experienced world (bhogya-prapañca) must also be false. In a dream where one sees oneself crowned king, one's royal retinue and the like cannot be real. Therefore, when enjoyership is terminated by Brahman-knowledge, the cessation of the objective world of experience also takes place. The entire insentient world is indeed an object of enjoyment (bhogya). Since the sūtra indicates its cessation through knowledge, it follows that the world is an effect of ignorance, unreal, and false (mithyā).

10கீதாபாஷ்யம், பஞ்சபாதிகா, பஞ்சபாதிகா விவரணம் முதலிய க்ரந்தங்களில் இந்தக் கருத்துகள் காணப்படுகின்றன.

These ideas are found in texts such as the Gītā-bhāṣya, Pañcapādikā, and Pañcapādikā-vivaraṇa.

गीताभाष्यम् ९-१०ततश्च एकस्य देवस्य सर्वाध्यक्षभूतचैतन्यमात्रस्य परमार्थतः सर्वभोगानभिसम्बन्धिनोऽन्यस्य चेतनान्तरस्याभावे भोक्तुरन्यस्याभावात् किन्निमित्तेयं सृष्टिः इति प्रश्नप्रतिवचने अनुपपन्ने॥ (भ. गी. भा - 9-10)

"And therefore, since there is only one God, of the nature of pure consciousness which is the witness of all, in reality unconnected with any enjoyment, and since there is no other conscious entity, there being no other experiencer, the question and answer regarding 'For what purpose is this creation?' become untenable" (Gītā-bhāṣya 9.10).

पञ्चपादिकापञ्चपादिका — तेन सूत्रकारेणैव ब्रह्मज्ञानं अनर्थहेतुनिबर्हणं सूत्रयता अविद्याहेतुकं कर्तृत्वभोक्तृत्वं प्रदर्शितं भवति। “यदैव अहङ्कर्ता अध्यासात्मकः तदैव तदुपकरणस्यापि तदात्मकत्वसिद्धिः। न हि स्वप्नावाप्तराज्याभिषेकस्य माहेन्द्रजालनिर्मितस्य वा राज्ञः राज्योपकरणं परमार्थसत् भवति”

Pañcapādikā: "By that, the author of the sūtras himself, while aphorising that the knowledge of Brahman destroys the cause of evil (anartha), shows that agency and enjoyership are caused by ignorance (avidyā). 'When the ego-agent itself is of the nature of superimposition (adhyāsa), then its accessories are also established to be of the same nature. For the royal paraphernalia of a king who has attained coronation in a dream, or one created by sorcery, is never ultimately real (paramārtha-sat)'."

पञ्चपादिकाविवरणम्पञ्चपादिकाविवरणम् — “शरीरादिपदार्थस्वरूपस्य मिथ्याकल्पित कर्तृ, भोक्तृ, प्रमातृ, दोषसंयुक्तात्मार्थतया कार्यतया, भोग्यतया, प्रमेयतया, द्वेष्यतया च तादर्थ्येनैव सृष्टत्वात् स्वरूपेणापि मिथ्यात्वं स्वप्नदृष्टान्तेन अनुमातुं शक्यते”

Pañcapādikā-vivaraṇa: "Because the nature of entities such as the body is created solely for the sake of the Self associated with the falsely superimposed defects of agency, enjoyership, and knowership—as its effects, objects of enjoyment, objects of knowledge, and objects of aversion—its falsity even in its own nature can be inferred through the example of a dream."

IVDefinition 4: appearing only where it is absolutely absent

IV. स्वात्यन्ताभावाधिकरणे एव प्रतीयमानत्वम्।

IV. [Falsity is] being perceived in the very locus of one's own absolute non-existence (svātyantābhāvādhikaraṇa eva pratīyamānatvam).

தான் உண்மையில் இல்லாத இடத்தில் தான் இருப்பதாகத் தோன்றுவது மித்யாத்வம் என்று ஸ்ரீ சித்சுகாசார்யர் அபிப்ராயப்படுவதாக அத்வைத ஸித்தி - லகு சந்த்ரிகை கூறுகிறது.

The Advaita-siddhi and Laghu-candrikā state that Śrī Citsukhācārya considers falsity (mithyātva) to be: appearing to exist in the very locus where it is in reality absent.

आत्मनिचैवं विचित्राश्चहि (ब्र. सू 2-1-28) என்ற ब्रह्म ஸூத்ரத்தில் ப்ரபஞ்சத்திற்கு இத்தகைய மித்யாத்வம் கூறப்படுகிறது. कृत्स्नप्रसक्तिर्निरवयवत्वशब्दकोपो वा (ब्र. सू - 2-1-26) என்ற இதற்கு முந்திய ஸூத்ரத்தில் ப்ருஹ்மம் ப்ரபஞ்ச ரூபமாக ஆக முடியாது என்ற ஆக்ஷேபம் காணப்படுகிறது. आत्मनिचैवं என்ற ஸூத்ரம் இதற்கு பரம சமாதானத்தை கூறுகிறது. ப்ருஹ்மத்தைப் பற்றிய ஆக்ஷேபத்திற்கு ஆத்மாவை த்ருஷ்டாந்தமாகக் கூறி சமாதானம் கூறுகிறபடியால் ஆத்மா என்னும் சப்தத்தினால் ஜீவாத்மாவைதான் ஸூத்ரக்காரர் 11குறிப்பிடுகிறார். ப்ருஹ்மத்திற்கு தானே த்ருஷ்டாந்தமாகாது. ஸ்வப்னத்தில் ஜீவாத்மாவானவன் தன்னை பல ரூபமாகவும் பலபோக்ய பதார்த்தங்களை அனுபவிப்பவனாகவும் காண்கிறான். இவனுக்கு ஏற்பட்ட பல ரூபங்களும், இவன் கண்ட பல போக்ய பதார்த்தங்களும் உண்மையில் இல்லை. இவைகள் இல்லாத இடத்திலேயே இவைகள் இருப்பதாகக் காண்கிறான். ப்ருஹதாரண்யக ச்ருதியும், ஸ்வப்னத்தில் न तत्र रथा न रथयोगा न पन्थानो भवन्ति என்ற வாக்யத்தினால் ரதங்கள் முதலியன இல்லை என்று முதலில் அறிவித்து விட்டு, பிறகு अथ रथान् रथयोगान् पथः सृजते (बृ. ४-३-१०) என்ற வாக்யத்தினால் ரதங்கள் முதலியன ஸ்ருஷ்டிக்கப்படுகின்றன என்று கூறுகிறது. இல்லாத ரூபங்களையும், பதார்த்தங்களையும் இருப்பதாக அனுபவிக்கும் ஸ்வப்ன நிலையில் உள்ள ஜீவாத்மாவை ப்ரஹ்மத்திற்கு த்ருஷ்டாந்தமாகக் கூறி அதுபோல் ப்ரஹ்மத்திலும் இல்லாத ப்ரபஞ்சமே இருப்பதாகத் தெரிகிறது என்று ஸூத்ரம் சொன்னதாக ஆகிறது. ஆகவே இல்லாத இடத்தில் இருப்பதாகத் தெரிவது மித்யாத்வம் என்று மேற்கூறப்பட்ட மித்யாத்வம் ப்ரபஞ்சத்திற்கு இந்த ஸூத்ரத்தால் தெரிவிக்கப்பட்டதாக ஆகிறது.

In the Brahma-sūtra "And thus in the Self, for there are diverse [creations]" (ātmani caivaṁ vicitrāśca hi, BS 2.1.28), this kind of falsity is stated for the world. In the preceding sūtra, "Either the entire [Brahman] would be transformed, or there would be contradiction of the scriptural texts declaring partlessness" (kṛtsnaprasaktir niravayavatva-śabda-kopo vā, BS 2.1.26), the objection is raised that Brahman cannot transform into the form of the world. The sūtra ātmani caivam provides the ultimate answer to this. Since the sūtrakāra answers the objection regarding Brahman by citing the self as an illustration, he refers only to the individual self (jīvātman) by the word ātman. Brahman itself cannot be its own illustration. In dream, the jīvātman sees itself assuming many forms and experiencing many objects of enjoyment. The many forms assumed by it and the many objects of enjoyment seen by it do not exist in reality. It sees them as existing in the very locus where they do not exist. The Bṛhadāraṇyaka śruti also, in the context of dream, first declares the absence of chariots and the like through the statement "There are no chariots there, no horses to be yoked to chariots, no roads" (na tatra rathā na rathayogā na panthāno bhavanti), and then says that chariots and the like are projected through the statement "Now he creates chariots, horses to be yoked, and roads" (atha rathān rathayogān pathaḥ sṛjate, Bṛ. Up. 4.3.10). By citing the jīvātman in the dream state—who experiences non-existent forms and objects as existing—as an example for Brahman, the sūtra implies that similarly, in Brahman too, the non-existent world merely appears to exist. Therefore, the aforementioned definition of falsity—namely, appearing to exist where it does not exist—is shown to be conveyed for the world by this sūtra.

VDefinition 5: other than the real

V. सद्भिन्नत्वं मिथ्यात्वम् (எது “ஸத்” இல்லையோ அது மித்யை).

V. Falsity is difference from the real (sad-bhinnatvaṁ mithyātvam), that is, whatever is not "real" (sat) is false (mithyā).

ஸ்ரீ ஆனந்தபோதாசார்யர் न्यायदीपावली என்ற க்ரந்தத்தில் सद्भिन्नत्वम्, अबाध्यभिन्नत्वम् என்று மித்யாத்வத்தைக் குறிப்பிடுகிறார். இந்த மித்யாத்வம் प्रमाणसिद्धभिन्नत्वम्— दोषजन्यज्ञानविषयभिन्नत्वम् என்று அத்வைத ஸித்தியில் விளக்கப்பட்டிருக்கிறது. எது தோஷத்தினால் தெரிகிறதோ அது மித்யை என்று இதனால் கூறப்பட்டதாக ஆகிறது. மேற்கூறிய आत्मनिचैवं என்ற ஸூத்ரம் நித்ரா தோஷத்தினால் காணப்படும் ஸ்வப்ன பதார்த்தங்களை த்ருஷ்டாந்தமாகக் கூறி ப்ரபஞ்ச ஸ்ருஷ்டியை உபபாதனம் செய்யும் மூலமாய் ப்ரபஞ்சம் தோஷ மூலகம் என்கிற இந்த மித்யாத்வத்திற்கும் ஆதாரமாகிறது.

Śrī Ānandabodhācārya, in the text Nyāya-dīpāvalī, mentions falsity as "distinct from the real" (sad-bhinnatvam) and "distinct from the unsublatable" (abādhya-bhinnatvam). This falsity is explained in the Advaita-siddhi as "distinct from what is established by valid means of knowledge (pramāṇa-siddha-bhinnatvam)—distinct from what is the object of a cognition produced by a defect (doṣa-janya-jñāna-viṣaya-bhinnatvam)". Through this, it is stated that whatever appears on account of a defect is false. The aforementioned sūtra ātmani caivam, by citing dream objects seen due to the defect of sleep as an illustration to explain cosmic creation, serves as the basis for this falsity as well, namely, that the world is rooted in defect (doṣa-mūlaka).

இதர ப்ரகாரங்கள்Further ways the sūtras teach mithyātva: jīva–brahma identity (2.1.14), the worldly and scriptural standpoints, the critique of other schools

12இதைத் தவிர, வேறு ப்ரகாரமாகவும் ஸூத்ரங்களில் ப்ரபஞ்ச மித்யாத்வம் கூறப்படுகிறது. அதாவது:—

Apart from this, the falsity of the world is also stated in the sūtras in another manner. That is to say:—

तदनन्यत्वं आरम्भणशब्दादिभ्यः (2-1-14) என்கிற ब्रह्म ஸூத்ரத்தில் आदि என்ற சப்தத்தால் जीवब्रह्मैक्यத்தைக் கூறுகின்ற வாக்யங்கள் குறிப்பிடப்படுகின்றன. அந்த ச்ருதி வாக்யங்களைக்கொண்டு तदनन्यत्वम् என்று जीवब्रह्मानन्यत्वम्— जीवब्रह्मैक्यम् ஸ்தாபிக்கப்படுகிறது. அங்குள்ள பாஷ்ய வசனம் இந்த அர்த்தத்தை தெரிவிக்கிறது:—

In the Brahma-sūtra "Non-otherness [of effect from cause] on account of the word 'origin' (ārambhaṇa) and the like" (tad-ananyatvam ārambhaṇa-śabdādibhyaḥ, BS 2.1.14), the word ādi ("and the like") indicates passages declaring the identity of the individual soul (jīva) and Brahman (jīva-brahmaikya). Taking those scriptural passages, non-difference (tad-ananyatva), which is the non-difference or identity of the jīva and Brahman (jīva-brahmānananyatva / jīva-brahmaikya), is established. The commentary (bhāṣya) passage there conveys this meaning:—

ब्रह्मसूत्रभाष्यम् २-१-१४आरम्भणशब्दादिभ्य इत्यादिशब्दात् ऐतदात्म्यमिदं सर्वं तत्सत्यं स आत्मा तत्वमसि (छा. ६-८-७) इदं सर्वं यदयमात्मा (बृ २-४-६) ब्रह्मैवेदं सर्वम् (मु. २-२-१२), आत्मैवेदं सर्वं (छा. ७-२५-२) इत्येवमाद्यप्यात्मैकत्वप्रतिपादनपरं वचनजातमुदाहर्तव्यम्। न चान्यथैकविज्ञानेन सर्वविज्ञानं संपद्यते। तस्माद्यथा घटकरकाद्याकाशानां महाकाशानन्यत्वं, यथा च मृगतृष्णिकोदकादीनामूषरादिभ्योऽनन्यत्वं, दृष्टनष्टस्वरूपत्वात् स्वरूपेण त्वनुपाख्यत्वात् एवमस्य भोग्यभोक्त्रादिप्रपञ्चजातस्य ब्रह्मव्यतिरेकेणाभावः इति द्रष्टव्यम्।

"By the word ādi ('etc.') in 'on account of words such as ārambhaṇa, etc.' (ārambhaṇa-śabdādibhyaḥ), the collection of passages aimed at propounding the oneness of the Self (ātmaikatva) should also be cited, such as: 'All this has this [Self] as its essence; that is the truth; that is the Self; that thou art' (aitadātmyam idaṃ sarvaṃ tat satyaṃ sa ātmā tat tvam asi, Ch. Up. 6.8.7); 'All this is what this Self is' (idaṃ sarvaṃ yad ayam ātmā, Bṛ. Up. 2.4.6); 'All this is verily Brahman' (brahmaivedaṃ sarvam, Mu. Up. 2.2.12); and 'All this is verily the Self' (ātmaivedaṃ sarvam, Ch. Up. 7.25.2). Nor could the knowledge of everything through the knowledge of the One be accomplished otherwise. Therefore, just as the spaces within a pot, water-jar, etc., are non-different from the vast expanse of space (mahākāśa), and just as the waters of a mirage, etc., are non-different from the saline desert, etc., because their nature is to appear and vanish (dṛṣṭa-naṣṭa-svarūpatvāt) and because in their own nature they are indescribable (svarūpeṇa tv anupākhyatvāt), so also the non-existence of this entire manifold of the world comprising the experienced object, the experiencer, etc. (bhogya-bhoktr-ādi-prapañca-jāta), apart from Brahman, should be understood."

இந்த ஸூத்ரத்தில் भोग्य ப்ரபஞ்சத்திற்கு ब्रह्मानन्यत्व- (ब्रह्मसत्तातिरिक्तसत्ताकत्वाभावः) மும் ஜீவனுக்கு (भोक्ता) ब्रह्मानन्यत्व (ब्रह्मैक्यं) மும் கூறப்படுகிறது. भोक्त्रापत्तेः என்ற முன் ஸூத்ரத்தில், “भोग्यं भोक्ता,” இவைகள் ப்ரஹ்ம ரூபம் என்று சொல்லப்பட்டது சரியல்ல, அப்படியானால் भोक्तृभोग्यविभागम् இல்லாமல் போய்விடும் என்று ஆக்ஷேபித்து भोक्ता, भोग्यं என்ற இரண்டையும் பற்றி லோகரீதியாக ஒரு சமாதானம் சொல்லிவிட்டு आरम्भणशब्दादिभ्यः என்று சாஸ்த்ர த்ருஷ்டியில் சமாதானம் கூறும் இந்த ஸூத்ரத்தில் भोक्ता, भोग्यं என்ற இரண்டிற்கும் तदनन्यत्वम् என்பதில் ப்ரம்ஹாபேதத்தை ஸ்தாபிப்பதுதான் மிகவும் பொருத்தமுடையதாக இருக்கிறது. इतरव्यपदेशात् என்ற அதிகரண ஸூத்ரத்தில் जीवब्रह्मैक्यத்தில் ஆக்ஷேப சமாதானம் காணுகிறபடியால், ஸூத்ரத்தில் இதற்கு முன்பு जीवब्रह्मैक्यं கூறப்பட்டிருக்க வேண்டும். भोग्यத்திற்கு மித்யாத்வமும், भोक्ता வுக்கு ब्रह्म13रूपत्वமும் கிடைப்பதற்காகவே மித்யாத்வம் என்று கூறாமல் अनन्यत्वम् என்று ஸூத்ரம் கூறுகிறது.

In this sūtra, non-otherness from Brahman (brahmānananyatva), defined as the absence of an existence apart from the existence of Brahman (brahma-sattātiriktasattākatvābhāva), is stated for the experienced world (bhogya), and non-otherness from Brahman, meaning identity with Brahman (brahmaikya), is stated for the soul (bhoktā). In the previous sūtra, "If it be argued that from [Brahman] becoming the experiencer..." (bhoktrāpatteḥ..., BS 2.1.13), an objection was raised: it is not correct to say that the experienced object (bhogya) and the experiencer (bhoktā) are forms of Brahman, because if that were so, the distinction between experiencer and experienced (bhoktṛ-bhogya-vibhāga) would cease to exist. Having given an empirical (laukika) solution concerning both the experiencer and the experienced, the present sūtra provides a solution from the scriptural standpoint (śāstra-dṛṣṭi) through ārambhaṇa-śabdādibhyaḥ; hence, it is most appropriate that tad-ananyatvam establishes non-difference from Brahman for both the experiencer (bhoktā) and the experienced (bhogya). Since objections and answers regarding the identity of the jīva and Brahman (jīva-brahmaikya) are found in the section sūtra beginning with "On account of the designation of the other..." (itaravyapadeśāt..., BS 2.1.21), the identity of the jīva and Brahman must have been stated prior to it in the sūtras. The sūtra uses the term "non-otherness" (ananyatva) rather than explicitly saying "falsity" (mithyātva) precisely so that falsity applies to the experienced (bhogya) while identity of nature with Brahman (brahma-rūpatva) applies to the experiencer (bhoktā).

कल्पतरु, परिमळ க்ரந்தங்களில் இந்த அர்த்தம் ஸ்பஷ்ட மாக்கப்படுகிறது: -

This meaning is made clear in the texts Kalpataru and Parimala:—

कल्पतरुःकल्पतरु: — पूर्वसूत्रे कार्यकारणानन्यत्ववत् घटाकाशकल्प- जीवानामपि महाकाशोपमब्रह्मात्मैक्यमुक्तम्, तस्य हिताकरणाद्यनुपपत्तिभिः आक्षेपात् सङ्गतिः “इति।

Kalpataru: "In the previous sūtra, just as the effect is non-different from the cause, the oneness of the Self of the individual souls—who are comparable to the pot-ether—with Brahman, who is comparable to the great ether, was also stated. Its contextual connection (saṅgati) arises from the objection based on incongruities such as [Brahman] not doing what is beneficial [to itself]."

परिमलःपरिमले च — विषयवाक्यश्रुतसर्वविज्ञानप्रतिज्ञानिर्वाहार्थं ब्रह्मात्मैक्यमप्यत्र विवक्षितम्। अत एव सूत्रे जीवेष्वभेदं जडेषु तद्व्यतिरेकेणाभावञ्च सङ्ग्रहीतुं तदनन्यत्वम् इत्युक्तं “इति।

And in the Parimala: "For the sake of sustaining the proposition of the knowledge of all through the knowledge of one (sarvavijñāna-pratijñā) heard in the thematic passage (viṣaya-vākya), the non-difference of Brahman and the Self (brahmātmaikya) is also intended here. For this very reason, in order to encompass non-difference (abheda) in the case of individual souls (jīvas) and non-existence apart from It (tadvyatirekeṇa abhāva) in the case of insentient objects (jaḍas), the sūtra has stated 'non-otherness' (tadananyatvam)."

ஆக ச்ருதி ப்ரமாணத்தைக்கொண்டு जीवब्रह्मैक्यத்தை ஸ்தாபித்து, அதன் மூலமாகவும், प्रपञ्चमिथ्यात्वத்தை இந்த ஸூத்ரம் தெரிவிக்கிறது. तथाहि இந்த ஸூத்ரத்தில் कर्ताவாகவும், भोक्ताவாகவும் கருதப்படும் ஜீவன் உண்மையில் பிரஹ்மமே என்று கூறியிருப்பதால் கர்த்ருத்வ போக்த்ருத்வங்கள் மித்யையாகத் தான் இருக்கவேண்டும் என்று स आत्मा तत्वमसि श्वेतकेतो इति च शारीरस्य ब्रह्मभावोपदेशात्। स्वयंसिद्धं ह्येतत् शारीरस्य ब्रह्मत्वं उपदिश्यते न यत्नान्तरप्रसाध्यम्। अतश्चेदं शास्त्रीयं ब्रह्मात्मत्वं अवगम्यमानं स्वाभाविकस्य शारीरात्मत्वस्य बाधकं सम्पद्यते रज्वादि- बुद्धय इव सर्पादिबुद्धीनाम्। बाधिते च शारीरात्मत्वे तदाश्रयः समस्तः स्वाभाविको व्यवहारः बाधितो भवति, यत्प्रसिद्धये नानात्वांशोऽपरो ब्रह्मणः कल्प्येत என்ற பாஷ்யம் கூறுகிறது.

Thus, establishing the identity of the individual soul and Brahman (jīva-brahma-aikya) on the authority of scripture (śruti-pramāṇa), this sūtra also makes known the falsity of the world (prapañca-mithyātva) through that very means. To explain (tathāhi): since it is stated in this sūtra that the jīva, who is regarded as agent (kartṛ) and enjoyer/experiencer (bhoktṛ), is in reality Brahman alone, agency (kartṛtva) and enjoyership (bhoktṛtva) must indeed be false (mithyā); as the Bhāṣya states: "Because of the instruction of the embodied soul's nature as Brahman in texts like 'That is the Self, That thou art, O Śvetaketu' (Ch. Up. 6.8.7). For this nature of the embodied soul as Brahman is taught as self-established (svayaṃ-siddha), not as something to be accomplished by another effort. And hence, this scriptural identity with Brahman, when comprehended, becomes the sublater (bādhaka) of the innate sense of being an embodied self (śārīrātmatva), just as the cognition of a rope etc. is of the cognitions of a snake etc. And when the sense of being an embodied self is sublated, all natural empirical activity (svābhāvika-vyavahāra) based upon it is sublated, for the sake of explaining which a distinct part of diversity (nānātvāṃśa) in Brahman might otherwise be postulated."

இதைப்பற்றிய पञ्चपादिका, पञ्चपादिकाविवरण க்ரந்தங்களில் உள்ள வாக்கியங்களும் மூன்றாவது மித்யாத்வத்தில் எடுத்துக் காட்டப்பட்டிருக்கின்றன.

The passages regarding this from texts such as the Pañcapādikā and the Pañcapādikā-vivaraṇa have also been cited under the third definition of falsity (mithyātva).

மேலும், லோகத்ருஷ்டி, சாஸ்த்ரத்ருஷ்டி என்ற இரண்டு நிலைகளிலிருந்து ஸூத்ரங்களில் பலவிடங்களில் लोकवत्तु लीलाकैवल्यम् (2-1-33) देवादिवदपि लोके (2–1–25) என்றும் शास्त्रदृष्ट्या तूपदेशो वामदेववत् என்றும் சமாதானம் கூறியிருப்பதைக் காண்கிறோம். शास्त्रदृष्ट्या तु என்கிற இடத்தில் 14தேஹாத்ம த்ருஷ்டி லோகத்ருஷ்டியாகவும், ப்ரஹ்மாத்ம த்ருஷ்டி சாஸ்த்ர த்ருஷ்டியாகவும் கூறப்படுகிறது. भोक्तृभोग्य ப்ரபஞ்ச விஷயத்திலும் भोक्त्रापत्तेः......लोकवत् என்று லோகத்ருஷ்டியில் சமாதானம் கூறிவிட்டு சாஸ்த்ரத்ருஷ்டியில் சமாதானம் கூறுவது ப்ரகாராந்தரமாகத்தான் இருக்கவேண்டும். லோகத்ருஷ்டியில் ப்ரபஞ்சம் ஸத்யமாகத் தோன்றுகிறது. ஆகையால் சாஸ்த்ர த்ருஷ்டியில் கூறப்படுவது ப்ரபஞ்சத்தினுடைய மித்யாத்வம் என்பது தான் உசிதம்.

Furthermore, we see that in many places in the sūtras, explanations are given from two standpoints—the empirical viewpoint (loka-dṛṣṭi) and the scriptural viewpoint (śāstra-dṛṣṭi)—as in "Yet [creation is] mere sport, as in the world" (BS 2.1.33), "Even as gods and the like in the world" (BS 2.1.25), and "The instruction, however, is from the scriptural perspective, as in the case of Vāmadeva" (BS 1.1.30). In the context of "The scriptural perspective, however" (śāstradṛṣṭyā tu), the view identifying the body with the Self (dehātma-dṛṣṭi) is stated as the empirical viewpoint, while the view identifying Brahman with the Self (brahmātma-dṛṣṭi) is stated as the scriptural viewpoint. In the matter of the world of experiencer and experienced (bhoktṛ-bhogya-prapañca) as well, having given a reply from the empirical standpoint in "On account of the experiencer becoming [the experienced] ... as in the world" (BS 2.1.13), the reply given from the scriptural standpoint must be of an entirely different kind. From the empirical viewpoint, the world appears real. Therefore, what is declared from the scriptural viewpoint is properly the falsity (mithyātva) of the world.

2 வது அத்யாயம், 2 வது பாதத்தில் समवायाभ्युपगमाच्च साम्यादनवस्थितेः (2-2-13) विप्रतिषेधाच्चासमञ्जसम् (2-2-10) अन्तवत्वमसर्वज्ञता वा (2-2-41) என்ற ஸூத்ரங்களில் பரமதங்கள் மீது பல தோஷங்கள் ஸூத்ரகாரரால் கூறப்படுகின்றன. समवायसम्बन्धத்தைக் கூறும் மதத்தின் மீது சொல்லப்படும் अनवस्था முதலிய தோஷங்கள் எந்த சம்பந்தத்தைக் கூறும் மதத்திற்கும் பொருந்தும். दृग्दृश्य சம்பந்தத்தை அத்வைதிகளும் கூறவேண்டியிருக்கிறது. सांख्य மதத்தின் மீது विप्रतिषेधाच्च என்ற ஸூத்ரத்தில் கூறப்படும் தோஷம்போல் அத்வைதத்தின் மீது ப்ரத்யக்ஷாதி ப்ரமாணங்களினால் विप्रतिषेधम् என்ற தோஷம் கூறலாம். பரமதங்கள் மீது கூறும் தோஷங்கள் அத்வைதத்தின் மீதும் கேட்கலாம் என்றிருக்க, ஸூத்ரகாரர் பரமதங்கள் மீது தோஷங்கள் கூறுவதின் அபிப்ராயம் என்னவெனில் ப்ரபஞ்சத்தை ஸத்யம் என்று கூறும் பரமதங்களுக்கு அவைகள் உண்மையாகவே தோஷங்களாகின்றன. ஆனால் ப்ரபஞ்ச மித்யாத்வத்தைக்கூறும் அத்வைதத்திற்கு அவைகள் தோஷங்களாக ஆகாமல், அந்த மித்யாத்வத்தை ஸ்தாபிப்பதற்கே அனுகூலமாகின்றன. विप्रतिषेधाच्च என்ற ஸூத்ரத்தின் பாஷ்யத்தில் இந்த அபிப்ராயம் காணப்படுகிறது. ஸ்ரீ ஹர்ஷரும் खण्डनखण्डखाद्यத்தில் இந்தக் கருத்தை தெரிவிக்கிறார்:

In the second adhyāya (chapter), second pāda (quarter), in the sūtras, "And because of the admission of samavāya (inherence), there is infinite regress on account of equality" (samavāyābhyupagamāc ca sāmyād anavasthiteḥ, BS 2.2.13), "And on account of contradiction, it is inconsistent" (vipratiṣedhāc cāsamañjasam, BS 2.2.10), and "Either finiteness or non-omniscience" (antavattvam asarvajñatā vā, BS 2.2.41), many defects (doṣas) in rival doctrines are stated by the Sūtrakāra. Defects such as anavasthā (infinite regress) stated against the school that accepts the relation of inherence (samavāya-sambandha) would apply to any school that posits a relation. Advaitins too must speak of the relation between the seer and the seen (dṛg-dṛśya-sambandha). Just like the defect stated against the Sāṅkhya school in the sūtra vipratiṣedhāc ca, the defect of vipratiṣedha (contradiction) with perceptual and other means of knowledge (pratyakṣādi-pramāṇa) could be alleged against Advaita. While it is thus possible to raise against Advaita the very defects leveled against rival doctrines, what is the intention of the Sūtrakāra in stating defects against rival doctrines? It is that for rival schools which maintain that the world is real (satyam), these defects are genuinely flaws. But for Advaita, which asserts the falsity of the world (prapañca-mithyātva), they do not become flaws; rather, they are conducive to establishing that very falsity (mithyātva). This purport is found in the bhāṣya on the sūtra vipratiṣedhāc ca. Śrī Harṣa also expresses this idea in the Khaṇḍana-khaṇḍa-khādya:

खण्डनखण्डखाद्यम्“अनवस्थादयो दोषाः सत्तां निघ्नन्ति वस्तुनः। अद्वैतिनां ते सुहृदः प्रपञ्चे तत्प्रसञ्जकाः॥”

"Defects such as infinite regress (anavasthā) destroy the reality (sattā) of an entity. To the Advaitins they are friends, as they establish that very [falsity] in the world."

खण्डनखण्डखाद्यम्15“अभीष्टसिद्धावपि खण्डनानां अखण्डि राज्ञामिव नैवमाशा। तत्तानि कस्मान्न यथावदेव सैद्धान्तिकेऽप्यध्वनि योजयध्वम्”

"Even though our desired end is accomplished, our desire for refutations is not broken, like the ambition of kings. Therefore, why should you not apply them just as they are even to the path of your own established tenets (siddhānta)?"

இவ்விதம், ஸத்யம் ஒன்றே, அது ப்ரஹ்மரூபமான ஆத்மா தான் என்ற அத்வைத ஸித்தாந்தத்தின் முக்ய கொள்கை ஸித்திப்பதற்கு ஆவச்யகமான ப்ரபஞ்ச மித்யாத்வம் அத்வைத ஸித்தாந்தத்தின் மூலப்ரமாணமான உபநிஷத்துகளின் அர்த்தங்களை நிர்ணயம் செய்யும் ப்ரஹ்ம ஸூத்ரங்களில் இவ்வாறு கூறப்பட்டிருக்கிறது என்பது இதனால் தெளிவாக்கப்பட்டது.

முன்னுரைPreface and dedication (by the Sabhā)

1The Falsity of the World in the Brahma-sūtra

Foreword

traditional maxim“Brahman is real; the world is false; the individual self (jīva) is Brahman alone and not different.”

Brahman alone is real across all three periods of time (trikāla-satya); the world is false (mithyā); the jīva and Brahman are one and the same, not different. This indeed is the essence of the Advaita doctrine. Among these, if the realization that Brahman alone is real and that the jīva and Brahman are non-different is to become a matter of direct experience (anubhava), it is possible only if one understands that the world is mithyā. It is the Upaniṣads alone that teach us this matter. However, so that the truths may be understood clearly—since that cannot be achieved merely by reading the Upaniṣads alone—the work called the Brahma-sūtra was composed by Śrī Veda-vyāsa. Commentaries on this work have been written by adherents of various traditions. Śrī Ādi Śaṅkara Bhagavatpāda, renowned for propagating the Advaita tradition, has clearly brought to light the aforesaid truths in his commentary. But proponents of other traditions object that Śrī Veda-vyāsa has not spoken of the world being mithyā in the Brahma-sūtra, which is the foundational text elaborating the intent of the Upaniṣads, and that this is merely Śrī Śaṅkara's imagination. Taking the word mithyā to mean 'completely non-existent', they argue without understanding the tenets. Although no debate on this can arise after attaining oneness with Brahman, for faithful seekers (āstikas) who proceed along this path and endeavor to experience the bliss of Advaita (advaitānanda) through the path of the Upaniṣads by the grace of Guru and Īśvara, this Sabhā dedicates this small treatise at the feet of the Ācārya, clarifying what is meant by the world being mithyā and how it has been explained in various ways in the foundational text, the Brahma-sūtra itself. We request [readers] to read this and be benefited.

அவதாரிகைIntroduction: what advaita means, and the five definitions of mithyātva

3The Falsity of the World in the Brahma-sūtra

(S. R. Krishnamurthy Shastri)

Even before Śrī Parameśvara incarnated in the form of Śrīmat Ādi Śaṅkara Bhagavatpāda to propagate the Vedāntic philosophy known as Advaita Darśana throughout the world, many great sages (maharṣis) from beginningless time have designated Advaita Darśana (doctrine) alone as the Aupaniṣada Darśana (the philosophy of the Upaniṣads) in their texts. Subsequently as well, in many orthodox (āstika) and heterodox (nāstika) texts, especially in Jaina and Buddhist works, they refer to Advaita Darśana alone as the Aupaniṣada Darśana. From this it becomes evident that Advaita Darśana is that which has the Upaniṣads (Vedāntas) as its means of valid knowledge (pramāṇa). Taking the Upaniṣads alone as pramāṇa, Śrī Bhagavatpāda too elucidated the Advaita Darśana. In the Brahma-sūtra, Śrī Veda-vyāsa has determined the purport (tātparya) of the statements in these Upaniṣads by means of various logical arguments (nyāyas).

4Advaita means that the real entity (satya-vastu) is one alone. A doubt may arise: 'Are not many entities known through means of knowledge such as perception (pratyakṣa)?' To this, the resolution offered in the Advaita Siddhānta is as follows: The nature of the Self (ātma-svarūpa), which is self-luminous (svayaṃ-prakāśa) and of the nature of plenary bliss (pūrṇānanda-rūpa), is the one reality. This one alone appears as many. It is precisely because it is in reality one and appears as manifold that the name prapañca (world/diversification) has been applied to it. If in reality there were many things, the name prapañca would not be appropriate for it. Prapañca means expansion or manifold appearance (vistāra). It is proper to say that the one alone undergoes expansion. The presence of multiple things is not called 'being an expansion'. Therefore, perceived objects (dṛśyam), merely because they are perceived, need not be real. Just because a rope is perceived as a snake, the snake does not become real. Thus, the Advaita Siddhānta that reality is one alone suffers no damage whatsoever from worldly transactions (loka-vyavahāra) established through means of knowledge such as perception.

If that is so, if it is asked what the nature (svarūpa) of the appearing world (prapañca) is, the Vedāntas declare that the appearing world is not real (satya), but false (mithyā).

Explaining the falsity (mithyātva) of the world through the five major definitions given in ancient texts, we shall see below how such world-falsity is indicated in the Brahma-sūtras.

Those five definitions (lakṣaṇas) of mithyātva are: (1) sadasadvilakṣaṇatvam—being distinct from the real (sat) and the unreal (asat), i.e., that which cannot be determined as either existent or non-existent; (2) pratipannopādhau traikālika-niṣedha-pratiyogitvam—being the counter-positive of a negation across all three times in the very locus where it is perceived, i.e., not existing in reality in the very place where it is perceived to exist; (3) jñāna-nivartyatvam—sublatability by knowledge, i.e., ceasing to be through knowledge; (4) svātyantābhāvādhikaraṇa eva pratīyamānatvam—being perceived only in the locus of its own absolute non-existence, i.e., appearing to exist in the very place where it is in reality absent; (5) sad-bhinnatvam—being different from the real (sat), i.e., not being ultimately real (paramārtha-sat).

IDefinition 1: neither real nor unreal

I. Sadasadvilakṣaṇatvaṃ mithyātvam (Falsity is distinctness from the real and the unreal).

5In the commentary on the Adhyāsa-bhāṣya of the Brahma-sūtra, Śrī Padmapāda has stated: 'The word mithyā has two meanings—it denotes denial/concealment (apahnava-vacana), and it denotes indeterminability (anirvacanīyatā-vacana)' (Pañcapādikā). According to this, when it is said that the world is mithyā, it does not mean that the world does not exist (is void / śūnya); it means that the world is indeterminable (anirvacanīya). Therefore, as stated in the Pañcapādikā, falsity is indeterminability (anirvacanīyatvam). In the Advaita-siddhi of Śrī Madhusūdana Sarasvatī Svāmin as well as in Śrī Bhagavatpāda's commentary on the Tadananyatvādhikaraṇa, [it is shown that] therefore there is no contradiction whatsoever with the Advaita Siddhānta that reality is one. Anirvacanīyam does not mean that which cannot be defined (nirvacana) or cannot be explicitly stated; it only means that which cannot be categorized either as sat (real), asat (unreal), or sadasat (both real and unreal) (sad-bhinnaṃ, asad-bhinnaṃ, sadasad-bhinnaṃ ca yat tat anirvācyam). Without noticing what has been thus stated in ancient Advaita works, adherents of other schools criticize Advaitins, saying, 'Advaitins declare the world to be void (śūnya), like the Mādhyamika Buddhists,' and 'They say the world cannot be spoken of.' That this criticism is invalid will be clear from what has been said above.

The śruti passage 'There was neither the non-existent (nāsadāsīt) nor the existent (no sadāsīt)... there was darkness (tama āsīt)' (Ṛgveda 10.129.1, 3) declares this falsity (mithyātva). In this passage, it is said that in dissolution (pralaya) there was no sat, no asat, but darkness (tamas = the primordial material cause / prakṛti of the world, avidyā, māyā) was present. By this, it amounts to saying that the material cause of the world is neither sat nor asat, but anirvacanīya. When prakṛti, the cause of the world, is anirvacanīya, it is self-evident without needing to be stated separately that its effect, the world, is also anirvacanīya. This meaning is made clear in the commentary on the Tadananyatvādhikaraṇa (of the Brahma-sūtra) as well:

Śaṅkara, BS-bhāṣya 2.1.14'Name and form (nāmarūpe), imagined through ignorance (avidyā-kalpite), being, as it were, identical with the omniscient Lord (sarvajñasyeśvarasyātmabhūte iva), indeterminable either as that reality or as different from it (tattvānyatvābhyām anirvacanīye), the seed of the transmigratory universe (saṃsāra-prapañca-bīja-bhūte), are spoken of in śruti and smṛti as the power of illusion (māyā-śaktiḥ) and the primordial nature (prakṛtiḥ) of the omniscient Lord.' (BS bhāṣya 2.1.14)

6This falsity in the form of distinctness from the real and the unreal (sadasadvilakṣaṇatvam) is determined for the world in the Brahma-sūtras. To explain (tathāhi): Sat (the real) is that which is uncontradicted in all three times (trikālābādhya), truly existing, and never ceases to exist at any time. According to Advaita Siddhānta, this entity is indeed the nature of the Self (ātma-svarūpa). Asat (the unreal) is that which does not exist at any time, an absolute void (śūnya); and that which is both real and unreal is sadasat. The world is not of these three kinds, but is the fourth, anirvacanīya (indeterminable).

That the world is not sat (real/existent) is made known by the Brahma-sūtra: "There is, however, no origination of the Existent (sat), because of impossibility" (asambhavastu sato'nupapatteḥ, BS 2.3.9).

In this sūtra, it is stated that there is no origination (utpatti) for the entity called sat. For the world, on the other hand, origination is spoken of in the Upaniṣads. In the Brahma-sūtra as well, in the third and fourth pādas of the second adhyāya, it is established that the world has origination. The sūtras that establish that the entity sat has no origination, while the world does have origination, make it clear that the world is not sat.

7By the Brahma-sūtra, "Division, however, extends as far as modification, as in the world" (yāvad-vikāraṃ tu vibhāgo lokavat, BS 2.3.7), it is stated that all things that appear differentiated are modifications (vikāra). All entities in the world appear differentiated. Therefore, they are modifications. That all modifications are mithyā (false/illusory) is understood from the Brahma-sūtra, "Non-otherness [of cause and effect], on account of words such as ārambhaṇa (taking hold / originating), etc." (tad-ananyatvam ārambhaṇa-śabdādibhyaḥ, BS 2.1.14). Since this sūtra occurs in the context of inquiring into the cause of the world (jagat-kāraṇa-vicāra-prakaraṇa), tat ("that") means the cause of the world. Tadananyatvam ("their non-otherness") means that the world is in reality not distinct from the causal entity. As the reasoning for this, it is stated in the sūtra, ārambhaṇa-śabdādibhyaḥ ("from words such as ārambhaṇa, etc."). By the expression ārambhaṇa-śabda, the śruti text, "A modification is a grasping by speech, a mere name; the clay alone is real" (vācārambhaṇaṃ vikāro nāmadheyaṃ mṛttikety eva satyam, Ch. Up. 6.1.4), is cited. In this śruti text, taking clay and its modifications such as pots, etc. (ghaṭādi), it is stated that clay (the cause) alone is real (satyam). That is (arthāt), it is understood that all modifications (pots, etc.) are mithyā.

Since this sūtra, relying on the śruti passage that speaks of the falsity of modifications (vikāra-mithyātva), states tadananyatvam (non-otherness from that), it explains that all modifications lack a real independent existence distinct from the nature of the cause (kāraṇa-sattātiriktasattākatvābhāvaḥ); the cause alone is sat, and the modification is not sat. Therefore, since by the Brahma-sūtra yāvadvikārantu... it is said that the entire world perceived as distinct is a modification, and by the sūtra tadananyatvam... it is said that all modifications have no existence other than the existence of the cause, it is clear that the world, being a modification, is not sat.

The sūtra 'And because of the existence of the posterior [effect in the cause]' (sattvāccāvarasya, BS 2.1.16) and the bhāṣya [statement] 'And again, existence is one' (ekaṃ ca punaḥ sattvam) also explain that existence (sattva) does not belong to the effect; rather, the existence present in the cause alone appears as though belonging to the effect.

In this manner, we have explained through the sūtra that the world is not sat (real).

That the world is not asat (void / śūnya) also becomes evident through the sūtra.

The Brahma-sūtra "There is no non-existence [of external objects], because they are perceived" (nābhāva upalabdheḥ, BS 2.2.28) refutes the Buddhist (Vijñānavādin) school which asserts that the world, the object of perception (dṛśya), is void (śūnya). It is stated in the sūtra that precisely because the perceived world is an object of perception (dṛśya), it is not non-existent (asat), nor is it void (śūnya).

8The sūtras also refute the coexistence of contradictory attributes such as existence (satva) and non-existence (asatva). When the sūtra "Not in one [locus], on account of impossibility" (naikasminnasambhavāt, BS 2.2.33) is considered in conjunction with the subsequent sūtra "And likewise, there would be non-entirety of the Self" (evaṁ cātmākārtsnyam, BS 2.2.34), it is seen to refute the Jaina school, which asserts the coexistence of contradictory attributes through "maybe it is, maybe it is not" (syādasti syānnāsti). The bhāṣya statement, "Indeed, the simultaneous coexistence of contradictory properties such as existence and non-existence in a single substratum is impossible, like cold and heat" (na hyekasmin dharmiṇi yugapat sadasatvādi-viruddhadharma-samāveśaḥ sambhavati śītoṣṇavat), also makes it clear that the coexistence of contradictory properties is impossible. From this, it is clear that the world is not of the nature of being both real and unreal (sadasat).

Thus, the definition of falsity stated in the Pañcapādikā—"that which is distinct from the real (sad-bhinna), distinct from the unreal (asad-bhinna), and distinct from both real and unreal (sadasad-bhinna) is indeterminable (anirvācya), false (mithyā)"—is also indicated for the world in the sūtras.

IIDefinition 2: negated, in all three times, in the very place it appears

II. Falsity is being the counterpositive of a negation across all three divisions of time in the very locus where it is perceived (pratipannopādhau traikālika-niṣedha-pratiyogitvaṁ mithyātvam).

In the Pañcapādikā-vivaraṇa, falsity (mithyātva) is explained as: not existing in reality in the very locus where it appears to exist.

Brahma-sūtra 3.2.22"For [the text] denies the previously mentioned limited nature [of Brahman], and then speaks further" (prakṛtaitāvattvaṁ hi pratiṣedhati tato bravīti ca bhūyaḥ, BS 3.2.22).

By this Brahma-sūtra, this definition of falsity is explained with regard to the world. The śruti passage "Now therefore the teaching: 'Not this, not this'" (athāta ādeśo neti neti, Bṛ. Up. 2.3.6) is examined in this sūtra. In the earlier śruti passage, "There are indeed two forms of Brahman" (dve vāva brahmaṇo rūpe, Bṛ. Up. 2.3.1), the forms of the world are attributed to Brahman. In the passage "Not this, not this" (neti neti), these forms are negated. Since the śruti first indicates the presence of the world in Brahman and then declares that the world is not in Brahman, it is clear that the world does not truly exist in Brahman, which is its substratum of appearance. That this is the meaning of this śruti passage is conveyed by this sūtra. Since no specific time is mentioned in the statement neti neti, it is also evident that the world is not real across all three periods of time (past, present, future). Furthermore, because the śruti has acknowledged the cosmic forms in Brahman by saying dve vāva brahmaṇo rūpe, it is clear that the world is not void (śūnya / asat). Therefore, this sūtra establishes falsity in the sense of being the counterpositive of a negation across all three divisions of time in the very locus where it is perceived (pratipannopādhau traikālika-niṣedha-pratiyogitvam).

IIIDefinition 3: removable by knowledge

9III. Falsity is sublatability by knowledge (jñāna-nivartyatvaṁ mithyātvam).

The definition of falsity (mithyātva) as sublatability by knowledge (jñāna-nivartyatva)—that whatever is terminated by knowledge is false—is also elucidated in the Pañcapādikā-vivaraṇa.

For the world, this definition of falsity is conveyed in the Brahma-sūtra "Now, therefore, the inquiry into Brahman" (athāto brahma-jijñāsā, BS 1.1.1). This sūtra prescribes the knowledge of Brahman as the means for one who desires liberation (mokṣa). Liberation is the cessation of bondage (bandha-nivṛtti). The transmigratory dealings between birth and death are considered bondage. The cause for these is the actions of merit and demerit (puṇya-pāpa-karma). In brief, agency and enjoyership (kartṛtva-bhoktṛtva) constitute the jīva's bondage. That this bondage is terminated by the knowledge of Brahman is known from this sūtra, which declares knowledge of Brahman to be the means to liberation. It is universally accepted that only ignorance (ajñāna) is terminated by knowledge. Since the sūtra states that bondage is terminated by knowledge, it is certain that bondage is an effect of ignorance (ajñāna-kārya). It is well known that all effects of ignorance, such as the rope-snake (rajju-sarpa), are not real. Consequently, the bondage of the jīva, consisting of agency and enjoyership that can be terminated by Brahman-knowledge, is not real but false (mithyā). If enjoyership is false, the experienced world (bhogya-prapañca) must also be false. In a dream where one sees oneself crowned king, one's royal retinue and the like cannot be real. Therefore, when enjoyership is terminated by Brahman-knowledge, the cessation of the objective world of experience also takes place. The entire insentient world is indeed an object of enjoyment (bhogya). Since the sūtra indicates its cessation through knowledge, it follows that the world is an effect of ignorance, unreal, and false (mithyā).

10These ideas are found in texts such as the Gītā-bhāṣya, Pañcapādikā, and Pañcapādikā-vivaraṇa.

Śaṅkara, Gītā-bhāṣya 9.10"And therefore, since there is only one God, of the nature of pure consciousness which is the witness of all, in reality unconnected with any enjoyment, and since there is no other conscious entity, there being no other experiencer, the question and answer regarding 'For what purpose is this creation?' become untenable" (Gītā-bhāṣya 9.10).
Padmapāda, PañcapādikāPañcapādikā: "By that, the author of the sūtras himself, while aphorising that the knowledge of Brahman destroys the cause of evil (anartha), shows that agency and enjoyership are caused by ignorance (avidyā). 'When the ego-agent itself is of the nature of superimposition (adhyāsa), then its accessories are also established to be of the same nature. For the royal paraphernalia of a king who has attained coronation in a dream, or one created by sorcery, is never ultimately real (paramārtha-sat)'."
Prakāśātman, Pañcapādikā-vivaraṇaPañcapādikā-vivaraṇa: "Because the nature of entities such as the body is created solely for the sake of the Self associated with the falsely superimposed defects of agency, enjoyership, and knowership—as its effects, objects of enjoyment, objects of knowledge, and objects of aversion—its falsity even in its own nature can be inferred through the example of a dream."
IVDefinition 4: appearing only where it is absolutely absent

IV. [Falsity is] being perceived in the very locus of one's own absolute non-existence (svātyantābhāvādhikaraṇa eva pratīyamānatvam).

The Advaita-siddhi and Laghu-candrikā state that Śrī Citsukhācārya considers falsity (mithyātva) to be: appearing to exist in the very locus where it is in reality absent.

11In the Brahma-sūtra "And thus in the Self, for there are diverse [creations]" (ātmani caivaṁ vicitrāśca hi, BS 2.1.28), this kind of falsity is stated for the world. In the preceding sūtra, "Either the entire [Brahman] would be transformed, or there would be contradiction of the scriptural texts declaring partlessness" (kṛtsnaprasaktir niravayavatva-śabda-kopo vā, BS 2.1.26), the objection is raised that Brahman cannot transform into the form of the world. The sūtra ātmani caivam provides the ultimate answer to this. Since the sūtrakāra answers the objection regarding Brahman by citing the self as an illustration, he refers only to the individual self (jīvātman) by the word ātman. Brahman itself cannot be its own illustration. In dream, the jīvātman sees itself assuming many forms and experiencing many objects of enjoyment. The many forms assumed by it and the many objects of enjoyment seen by it do not exist in reality. It sees them as existing in the very locus where they do not exist. The Bṛhadāraṇyaka śruti also, in the context of dream, first declares the absence of chariots and the like through the statement "There are no chariots there, no horses to be yoked to chariots, no roads" (na tatra rathā na rathayogā na panthāno bhavanti), and then says that chariots and the like are projected through the statement "Now he creates chariots, horses to be yoked, and roads" (atha rathān rathayogān pathaḥ sṛjate, Bṛ. Up. 4.3.10). By citing the jīvātman in the dream state—who experiences non-existent forms and objects as existing—as an example for Brahman, the sūtra implies that similarly, in Brahman too, the non-existent world merely appears to exist. Therefore, the aforementioned definition of falsity—namely, appearing to exist where it does not exist—is shown to be conveyed for the world by this sūtra.

VDefinition 5: other than the real

V. Falsity is difference from the real (sad-bhinnatvaṁ mithyātvam), that is, whatever is not "real" (sat) is false (mithyā).

Śrī Ānandabodhācārya, in the text Nyāya-dīpāvalī, mentions falsity as "distinct from the real" (sad-bhinnatvam) and "distinct from the unsublatable" (abādhya-bhinnatvam). This falsity is explained in the Advaita-siddhi as "distinct from what is established by valid means of knowledge (pramāṇa-siddha-bhinnatvam)—distinct from what is the object of a cognition produced by a defect (doṣa-janya-jñāna-viṣaya-bhinnatvam)". Through this, it is stated that whatever appears on account of a defect is false. The aforementioned sūtra ātmani caivam, by citing dream objects seen due to the defect of sleep as an illustration to explain cosmic creation, serves as the basis for this falsity as well, namely, that the world is rooted in defect (doṣa-mūlaka).

இதர ப்ரகாரங்கள்Further ways the sūtras teach mithyātva: jīva–brahma identity (2.1.14), the worldly and scriptural standpoints, the critique of other schools

12Apart from this, the falsity of the world is also stated in the sūtras in another manner. That is to say:—

In the Brahma-sūtra "Non-otherness [of effect from cause] on account of the word 'origin' (ārambhaṇa) and the like" (tad-ananyatvam ārambhaṇa-śabdādibhyaḥ, BS 2.1.14), the word ādi ("and the like") indicates passages declaring the identity of the individual soul (jīva) and Brahman (jīva-brahmaikya). Taking those scriptural passages, non-difference (tad-ananyatva), which is the non-difference or identity of the jīva and Brahman (jīva-brahmānananyatva / jīva-brahmaikya), is established. The commentary (bhāṣya) passage there conveys this meaning:—

Śaṅkara, BS-bhāṣya 2.1.14"By the word ādi ('etc.') in 'on account of words such as ārambhaṇa, etc.' (ārambhaṇa-śabdādibhyaḥ), the collection of passages aimed at propounding the oneness of the Self (ātmaikatva) should also be cited, such as: 'All this has this [Self] as its essence; that is the truth; that is the Self; that thou art' (aitadātmyam idaṃ sarvaṃ tat satyaṃ sa ātmā tat tvam asi, Ch. Up. 6.8.7); 'All this is what this Self is' (idaṃ sarvaṃ yad ayam ātmā, Bṛ. Up. 2.4.6); 'All this is verily Brahman' (brahmaivedaṃ sarvam, Mu. Up. 2.2.12); and 'All this is verily the Self' (ātmaivedaṃ sarvam, Ch. Up. 7.25.2). Nor could the knowledge of everything through the knowledge of the One be accomplished otherwise. Therefore, just as the spaces within a pot, water-jar, etc., are non-different from the vast expanse of space (mahākāśa), and just as the waters of a mirage, etc., are non-different from the saline desert, etc., because their nature is to appear and vanish (dṛṣṭa-naṣṭa-svarūpatvāt) and because in their own nature they are indescribable (svarūpeṇa tv anupākhyatvāt), so also the non-existence of this entire manifold of the world comprising the experienced object, the experiencer, etc. (bhogya-bhoktr-ādi-prapañca-jāta), apart from Brahman, should be understood."

13In this sūtra, non-otherness from Brahman (brahmānananyatva), defined as the absence of an existence apart from the existence of Brahman (brahma-sattātiriktasattākatvābhāva), is stated for the experienced world (bhogya), and non-otherness from Brahman, meaning identity with Brahman (brahmaikya), is stated for the soul (bhoktā). In the previous sūtra, "If it be argued that from [Brahman] becoming the experiencer..." (bhoktrāpatteḥ..., BS 2.1.13), an objection was raised: it is not correct to say that the experienced object (bhogya) and the experiencer (bhoktā) are forms of Brahman, because if that were so, the distinction between experiencer and experienced (bhoktṛ-bhogya-vibhāga) would cease to exist. Having given an empirical (laukika) solution concerning both the experiencer and the experienced, the present sūtra provides a solution from the scriptural standpoint (śāstra-dṛṣṭi) through ārambhaṇa-śabdādibhyaḥ; hence, it is most appropriate that tad-ananyatvam establishes non-difference from Brahman for both the experiencer (bhoktā) and the experienced (bhogya). Since objections and answers regarding the identity of the jīva and Brahman (jīva-brahmaikya) are found in the section sūtra beginning with "On account of the designation of the other..." (itaravyapadeśāt..., BS 2.1.21), the identity of the jīva and Brahman must have been stated prior to it in the sūtras. The sūtra uses the term "non-otherness" (ananyatva) rather than explicitly saying "falsity" (mithyātva) precisely so that falsity applies to the experienced (bhogya) while identity of nature with Brahman (brahma-rūpatva) applies to the experiencer (bhoktā).

This meaning is made clear in the texts Kalpataru and Parimala:—

Amalānanda, KalpataruKalpataru: "In the previous sūtra, just as the effect is non-different from the cause, the oneness of the Self of the individual souls—who are comparable to the pot-ether—with Brahman, who is comparable to the great ether, was also stated. Its contextual connection (saṅgati) arises from the objection based on incongruities such as [Brahman] not doing what is beneficial [to itself]."
Appayya Dīkṣita, ParimalaAnd in the Parimala: "For the sake of sustaining the proposition of the knowledge of all through the knowledge of one (sarvavijñāna-pratijñā) heard in the thematic passage (viṣaya-vākya), the non-difference of Brahman and the Self (brahmātmaikya) is also intended here. For this very reason, in order to encompass non-difference (abheda) in the case of individual souls (jīvas) and non-existence apart from It (tadvyatirekeṇa abhāva) in the case of insentient objects (jaḍas), the sūtra has stated 'non-otherness' (tadananyatvam)."

Thus, establishing the identity of the individual soul and Brahman (jīva-brahma-aikya) on the authority of scripture (śruti-pramāṇa), this sūtra also makes known the falsity of the world (prapañca-mithyātva) through that very means. To explain (tathāhi): since it is stated in this sūtra that the jīva, who is regarded as agent (kartṛ) and enjoyer/experiencer (bhoktṛ), is in reality Brahman alone, agency (kartṛtva) and enjoyership (bhoktṛtva) must indeed be false (mithyā); as the Bhāṣya states: "Because of the instruction of the embodied soul's nature as Brahman in texts like 'That is the Self, That thou art, O Śvetaketu' (Ch. Up. 6.8.7). For this nature of the embodied soul as Brahman is taught as self-established (svayaṃ-siddha), not as something to be accomplished by another effort. And hence, this scriptural identity with Brahman, when comprehended, becomes the sublater (bādhaka) of the innate sense of being an embodied self (śārīrātmatva), just as the cognition of a rope etc. is of the cognitions of a snake etc. And when the sense of being an embodied self is sublated, all natural empirical activity (svābhāvika-vyavahāra) based upon it is sublated, for the sake of explaining which a distinct part of diversity (nānātvāṃśa) in Brahman might otherwise be postulated."

The passages regarding this from texts such as the Pañcapādikā and the Pañcapādikā-vivaraṇa have also been cited under the third definition of falsity (mithyātva).

14Furthermore, we see that in many places in the sūtras, explanations are given from two standpoints—the empirical viewpoint (loka-dṛṣṭi) and the scriptural viewpoint (śāstra-dṛṣṭi)—as in "Yet [creation is] mere sport, as in the world" (BS 2.1.33), "Even as gods and the like in the world" (BS 2.1.25), and "The instruction, however, is from the scriptural perspective, as in the case of Vāmadeva" (BS 1.1.30). In the context of "The scriptural perspective, however" (śāstradṛṣṭyā tu), the view identifying the body with the Self (dehātma-dṛṣṭi) is stated as the empirical viewpoint, while the view identifying Brahman with the Self (brahmātma-dṛṣṭi) is stated as the scriptural viewpoint. In the matter of the world of experiencer and experienced (bhoktṛ-bhogya-prapañca) as well, having given a reply from the empirical standpoint in "On account of the experiencer becoming [the experienced] ... as in the world" (BS 2.1.13), the reply given from the scriptural standpoint must be of an entirely different kind. From the empirical viewpoint, the world appears real. Therefore, what is declared from the scriptural viewpoint is properly the falsity (mithyātva) of the world.

In the second adhyāya (chapter), second pāda (quarter), in the sūtras, "And because of the admission of samavāya (inherence), there is infinite regress on account of equality" (samavāyābhyupagamāc ca sāmyād anavasthiteḥ, BS 2.2.13), "And on account of contradiction, it is inconsistent" (vipratiṣedhāc cāsamañjasam, BS 2.2.10), and "Either finiteness or non-omniscience" (antavattvam asarvajñatā vā, BS 2.2.41), many defects (doṣas) in rival doctrines are stated by the Sūtrakāra. Defects such as anavasthā (infinite regress) stated against the school that accepts the relation of inherence (samavāya-sambandha) would apply to any school that posits a relation. Advaitins too must speak of the relation between the seer and the seen (dṛg-dṛśya-sambandha). Just like the defect stated against the Sāṅkhya school in the sūtra vipratiṣedhāc ca, the defect of vipratiṣedha (contradiction) with perceptual and other means of knowledge (pratyakṣādi-pramāṇa) could be alleged against Advaita. While it is thus possible to raise against Advaita the very defects leveled against rival doctrines, what is the intention of the Sūtrakāra in stating defects against rival doctrines? It is that for rival schools which maintain that the world is real (satyam), these defects are genuinely flaws. But for Advaita, which asserts the falsity of the world (prapañca-mithyātva), they do not become flaws; rather, they are conducive to establishing that very falsity (mithyātva). This purport is found in the bhāṣya on the sūtra vipratiṣedhāc ca. Śrī Harṣa also expresses this idea in the Khaṇḍana-khaṇḍa-khādya:

Śrī Harṣa, Khaṇḍana-khaṇḍa-khādya"Defects such as infinite regress (anavasthā) destroy the reality (sattā) of an entity. To the Advaitins they are friends, as they establish that very [falsity] in the world."
உபஸம்ஹாரம்Conclusion
15Śrī Harṣa, Khaṇḍana-khaṇḍa-khādya"Even though our desired end is accomplished, our desire for refutations is not broken, like the ambition of kings. Therefore, why should you not apply them just as they are even to the path of your own established tenets (siddhānta)?"

In this manner, it has been clarified herein that the falsity of the world (prapañca-mithyātva)—which is indispensable for establishing the cardinal doctrine of Advaita Vedānta that Reality is one alone, and that it is the Self identical with Brahman (brahma-rūpa-ātman)—is expounded in these various ways in the Brahma-sūtras, which determine the meanings of the Upaniṣads, the fundamental source of knowledge (mūla-pramāṇa) for the Advaita doctrine.

May auspiciousness prevail (śubham).
