संचारपूतानि दिगन्तराणि कुर्वन् विवृण्वन्निगमार्थतत्त्वम् ।
संशोधयन्सर्वजनान्तरङ्गं त्वं स्मारयस्यादिजगद्गुरु नः ॥
अविरतं परकार्यकृतां सतां मधुरिमातिशयेन वचोऽमृतम् ।
अपि च मानसमम्बुनिधिर्यशो विमलशारदचन्द्रचन्द्रिका ॥
Hallowing the far quarters of the earth by your travels, unfolding the true essence of the Vedas, and purifying the innermost being of all people — you, O foremost world-teacher (ādi-jagadguru), call [the first Śaṅkara] to our remembrance.
Among the holy who labour unceasingly for others’ good, your speech is nectar by its surpassing sweetness, your mind a fathomless ocean, and your fame the moonlight of the spotless autumn moon.

109 · ग्रन्थारम्भप्रतिज्ञा
यस्त्वनुबन्धचतुष्टयं सम्यक् विज्ञाय वेदान्तविज्ञानसुनिश्चितार्थं स्थितप्रज्ञं वरिष्ठं श्रोत्रियं सद्गुरुमासाद्य तत्प्रसादाद्वेदान्तग्रन्थं पठति, श‍ृणोति चात्यादरेण श्रद्धया भक्त्या च, स एव मोक्षसाधनं ज्ञानं प्राप्नोति ।
He who, having thoroughly understood the fourfold prerequisites (anubandha-catuṣṭaya), approaches a true Guru who is versed in the Vedas, established in wisdom, the most eminent, and fully ascertained in the truth of Vedānta, and by his grace reads and listens to the Vedānta treatise with deep reverence, faith, and devotion, he alone attains the knowledge that is the means to liberation.
गुरुशिष्यसंवादरूपेणार्थनिरूपणे कृते श्रोतॄणां सुखावबोधो भवेदिति गुरुशिष्यप्रश्नप्रतिवचनरूपेण ग्रन्थ आरभ्यते ।
Thinking that listeners will have an easy understanding if the exposition of the truth is made in the form of a dialogue between guru and disciple, the work is begun in the form of questions and answers between guru and disciple.
110 · गुरुलक्षणं
यः साङ्गवेदाध्यायी तदर्थज्ञो जीवब्रह्मैक्यविषयकदृढतरनिश्चयात्परिनिष्ठितात्मसाक्षात्कारवान् स एव गुरुः ।
He who studies the Vedas with their auxiliary branches, knows their meaning, and possesses a firmly established direct realization of the Self arising from a steadfast conviction regarding the identity of jīva and Brahman, he alone is a Guru.
न हि वेदाध्ययनवत्त्वमात्रेणात्मज्ञानशून्यो गुरुर्भवति ।
For one who is devoid of Self-knowledge does not become a Guru merely by virtue of having studied the Vedas.
आत्मज्ञोऽपि वेदाध्ययनशून्यः, स्वयं मुक्तोऽपि परोपदेशयोग्यगुरुर्न स्यात्; यतो जिज्ञासुशिष्यहृद्गतसन्देहनिवर्तनक्षमाणां युक्तीनां प्रतिभानं वेदाध्ययनशून्यस्य न जायते ।
Even a knower of the Self, if devoid of Vedic study, though liberated himself, cannot be a Guru qualified to instruct others; because the intuitive grasp of logical arguments capable of resolving the doubts situated in the heart of an inquiring disciple does not arise in one who has not studied the Vedas.
सन्देहशून्यस्योत्तमसंस्कारवतश्चरमजन्मनो जिज्ञासोरुपदेशे समर्थोऽप्ययम्, साधारण्येन सर्वमुमुक्षुजनोपदेशसामर्थ्याभावान्नाचार्यो भवितुमर्हति ।
Although such a person may be competent to instruct an inquirer who is free from doubts, possesses the highest saṃskāras, and is in his final birth, he does not deserve to be an Ācārya due to the lack of capacity to instruct all seekers of liberation in general.
तस्माद्वेदाध्ययनसम्पन्न आत्मज्ञश्चैवाचार्य इत्युच्यते ।
Therefore, only one who is both endowed with Vedic learning and a knower of the Self is called an Ācārya.
स हि शिष्यबुद्धिगतपञ्चविधभेदान्नानायुक्तिभिर्निवर्तयितुं समर्थः स्यात् ।
For he is capable of dispelling, through diverse reasonings, the five kinds of differences seated in the intellect of the disciple.
ते च भेदाः - (१) जीवेश्वरभेदः, (२) जीवानां परस्परभेदः, (३) जीवजडभेदः, (४) ईश्वरजडभेदः, (५) जडानां परस्परभेद इति ।
And those differences are: (1) the difference between jīva and Īśvara, (2) the mutual difference among jīvas, (3) the difference between jīva and insentient matter, (4) the difference between Īśvara and insentient matter, and (5) the mutual difference among insentient entities.
सर्वानेतान् भेदान् खण्डयितुं स एव समर्थः ।
He alone is capable of refuting all these differences.
भेदश्1,2च
And difference [is the cause of fear]...
टिप्पणी
1.
यथा स्वयं मार्गज्ञो मार्गोपदेशं समर्थस्तथा ब्रह्मस्वरूपज्ञ एव ब्रह्मोपदेशयोग्यो भवति ।
Just as one who personally knows the path is competent to instruct about the path, similarly only one who knows the nature of Brahman is qualified to give instruction on Brahman.
2.
पञ्चभेदखण्डनयुक्तयः- (१) घटाकाशमठाकाशभेदवत् जीवेश्वरभेदोऽविद्यामायारूपोपाधिजनितत्वात् कल्पितः ।
The arguments for refuting the five differences: (1) Like the difference between the space in a pot (ghaṭākāśa) and the space in a hall (maṭhākāśa), the difference between jīva and Īśvara is imagined/illusory because it is generated by the limiting adjuncts in the form of avidyā and māyā.
(२) नानाघटाकाशभेदवत् जीवानां परस्परभेद आभाससहितान्तःकरणरूपोपाधिजनितत्वात् कल्पितः ।
(2) Like the mutual difference among various pot-spaces, the mutual difference among jīvas is illusory because it is produced by the limiting adjuncts in the form of internal organs (antaḥkaraṇa) possessing the reflection of consciousness (ābhāsa).
(३) जीवजडभेदः साभासान्तःकरणनिराभासनामरूपमायोपाधिजनितत्वात् कल्पितः ।
(3) The difference between jīva and inert matter is illusory because it is produced by the limiting adjuncts, namely, the internal organ with reflection (for jīva) and the reflectionless name-and-form māyā (for matter).
(४) ईश्वरजडभेदः साभासमायानामरूपमायोपाधिजनितत्वात् कल्पितः ।
(4) The difference between Īśvara and inert matter is illusory because it is produced by the limiting adjuncts: māyā with reflected consciousness (for Īśvara) and reflectionless name-and-form māyā (for inert matter).
(५) रज्ज्वारोपितसर्पदण्डादिभेदवत् जडानां परस्परभेदो नामरूपमायोपाधिजनितत्वात् कल्पितः ।
(5) Like the mutual difference between a snake, a stick, etc., superimposed on a rope, the mutual difference among inert objects is illusory because it is produced by the limiting adjunct of name-and-form māyā.
भयहेतुः ।
[Difference is] the cause of fear.
उदरमन्तरं कुरुते (तै. ब्र. ७) द्वितीयाद्वै भयं भवति (बृ. १.४.२) इत्यादिश्रुतिभ्यः ।
This is established from Śruti texts such as: 'He who makes even a slight distinction [finds fear]' (Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa 7) and 'Fear arises indeed from a second' (Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 1.4.2).
तस्माद्भेदोऽवश्यं खण्डनीयः ।
Therefore, difference must necessarily be refuted.
उक्तलक्षण एवाचार्यः स्वशिष्यं अविद्यादिसर्वमलशून्याद्वितीयब्रह्मैवाहमस्मि इति साक्षादपरोक्षानुभववन्तं कर्तुं शक्नुयात् ।
Only an Ācārya endowed with the stated characteristics can make his disciple possess the direct and immediate experience that 'I am indeed the non-dual Brahman, devoid of all impurities such as avidyā'.
तादृशसाक्षात्कारवानेव शिष्यः, सर्वोऽपि संसारो दृश्यप्रपञ्चो द्वैतः स्वप्नादिवन्मयि चिन्मात्रे स्वाज्ञानवशान्मिथ्या प्रतिभासते इति साक्षाज्जानीयात् ।
Only a disciple endowed with such realization directly knows that 'All saṃsāra, the perceived dual universe, falsely appears like a dream in me, Pure Consciousness alone, due to my own ignorance'.
ईदृशाद्भुतदुर्दर्शस्वात्मतत्त्वोपदेष्टैव आचार्य इत्युच्यते ।
He alone who teaches such a wondrous, difficult-to-perceive truth of one's own Self is called an Ācārya.
संसारमहाग्राहग्रस्तं शिष्यं तन्मुखाज्जीवब्रह्माद्वैतोपदेशेन यो मोचयति स एव गुरुः ।
He who liberates from its mouth the disciple caught by the great crocodile of worldly existence (saṃsāra) by instructing the non-duality of jīva and Brahman, he alone is a Guru.
बहवो गुरवः सन्ति शिष्यवित्तापहारकाः । दुर्लभोऽयं गुरुर्देवि शिष्यहृत्तापहारकः ॥ (गु. गी. १६०) इति स्मृतेः ।
For the Smṛti states: 'There are many gurus who snatch away the disciple's wealth. Rare, O Goddess, is that guru who removes the burning torment of the disciple's heart' (Guru Gītā 160).
111 · शिष्यलक्षणं
वेदान्तग्रन्थप्रवृत्तिसम्पादकं प्रथमतरङ्गे यदधिकारिविशेषणमुक्तं साधनचतुष्टयरूपं तत्सम्पत्तिरेव शिष्यस्य लक्षणं भवति ।
The acquisition of that very fourfold qualification (sādhana-catuṣṭaya), which was described in the first Taraṅga as the qualification of the eligible aspirant that brings about engagement with the Vedānta texts, constitutes the defining qualification of a disciple.
112 · गुरुभक्तेः फलं
ईश्वरापेक्षयाप्यधिकतया श्रद्धाभक्तिप्रणामादिकं गुरौ शिष्येण कर्तव्यम् ।
Faith, devotion, prostrations, and the like ought to be performed towards the Guru by the disciple even in greater measure than in comparison to Īśvara.
यतोऽखिलशास्त्रव्युत्पन्नोऽपि गुरूपदेशमन्तरा स्वात्मतत्त्वं न जानीयात् ।
Because even one who is proficient in all scriptures cannot know the truth of one's own Self without the Guru's instruction.
नैषा तर्केण मतिरापनेया (क. १.२.९) नायमात्मा प्रवचनेन लभ्यो न मेधया न बहुना श्रुतेन (क. १.२.२३, मु. ३.२.३) आचार्यवान् पुरुषो वेद (छा. ६.१४.२) आचार्याद्धैव विद्या विदिता साधिष्ठं प्रापत् (छा. ४.९.३) इति श्रुतेः ।
Owing to Śruti statements such as: This wisdom cannot be attained by mere logic (Kaṭha 1.2.9), This *Ātman* cannot be attained by discourse, nor by intellect, nor by much learning (Kaṭha 1.2.23, Muṇḍaka 3.2.3), The person having an *ācārya* knows (Chāndogya 6.14.2), and Knowledge learned only from the *ācārya* leads most effectively to the highest good (Chāndogya 4.9.3).
अयमेवार्थो वक्ष्यमाणदृष्टान्तेन स्फुटीक्रियते ।
This very point is clarified by the upcoming illustration.
तथा हि, वेदरूपसमुद्रो गुरूपदेशाभावे क्षारो भवति ।
To explain: the ocean of the Vedas becomes saline in the absence of the Guru's instruction.
क्षारसागरे पतितस्तज्जलं पीत्वा केवलं दुःखमेवानुभवति यथा, तथा गुरूपदेशं विनैव शास्त्रान्तरकुशला अपि स्वातन्त्र्येण वेदार्थं विचारयन्तो गुरुसम्प्रदायाभावाद्भेद एव सर्ववेदार्थ इति स्वमतिविभवानुसारेण निश्चित्य भेदरूपक्षारजलमेवास्वाद्य जननमरणप्रबन्धात्मकदुःखमेवाविश्रममनुभवन्ति; न तु मुक्तिरूपनित्यनिरतिशयानन्दं लभन्ते ।
Just as one who falls into a salt ocean and drinks its water experiences only distress, so too, even those proficient in other sciences who independently deliberate on the meaning of the Vedas without the instruction of a Guru, lacking traditional lineage, conclude according to their own intellect that duality alone is the purport of all Vedas; tasting only the brackish water of duality, they ceaselessly experience the misery consisting of the unbroken chain of birth and death, but do not obtain the eternal, unsurpassed *ānanda* of *mokṣa*.
टिप्पणी
1.
जननमरणात्मकं भयम् ।
The fear consisting of birth and death.
2.
श्रीवासिष्ठद्विविधभागवतसूतसंहितादिसकलेतिहासपुराणादिकेष्वद्वैतमेव प्रतिपाद्यते ।
In all *Itihāsa*s and *Purāṇa*s, including the *Yogavāsiṣṭha*, the two *Bhāgavata*s, the *Sūta-saṁhitā*, and others, non-duality (*Advaita*) alone is expounded.
तथाष्टोत्तरशतोपनिषतस्वपि ।
Similarly, in the one hundred and eight Upaniṣads as well.
अपि चानधिगताबाधितापूर्वत्वाच्चाद्वैतमेव शास्त्रार्थः ।
Moreover, because it is unacquired through other means, uncontradicted, and novel, non-duality (*Advaita*) alone is the true import of the scripture.
किञ्च बृह बृहि वृद्धौ इति धातुर्वृद्धिमाचष्टे ।
Furthermore, the verbal root bṛh / bṛhi vṛddhau signifies greatness / growth.
सा च वृद्धिरत्र प्रतियोगिविशेषानुपादानान्निरतिशयैव विवक्षिता ।
And here, since no specific limiting counterpart is specified, that greatness is intended to be absolutely unsurpassed (infinite).
सति तु वस्त्वन्तरे तेन परिच्छेदाद्वृद्धेर्निरतिशयत्वं भज्यते ।
If, however, another object exists, the unsurpassable nature of greatness is compromised due to limitation caused by that object.
तथा च वस्त्वन्तरकृतपरिच्छेदरहितमेव ब्रह्मशब्दवाच्यम् ।
Thus, that which is free from limitation caused by any second entity is alone the primary meaning of the word *Brahman*.
देशकालयोरपि वस्तुत्वात्, वस्तुमात्रस्य च ब्रह्मस्वरूपेऽध्यस्तत्वेन, वस्तुकृतपरिच्छेदनिरासेनैव ब्रह्मणि देशकालकृतपरिच्छेदनिरासो बोध्यः ।
Since space and time are also objects, and all objects are superimposed on the nature of *Brahman*, the negation of spatial and temporal limitations in *Brahman* is to be understood through the very negation of objective limitation (*vastu-pariccheda*).
जीवब्रह्मैक्योपदेष्टैव गुरुरिति गुरुलक्षणं निरूपितम्; तादृशादखण्डब्रह्मात्मैकत्वविदः सद्गुरोर्मुखाद्ये वेदान्तविचारं कुर्वन्ति, ते देवामृतादप्यधिकं निरतिशयं ब्रह्मानन्दमनुभवन्ति ।
The definition of a Guru has been stated as 'only the instructor of the identity of *jīva* and *Brahman*'; those who engage in Vedāntic inquiry from the mouth of such a true Guru who knows the indivisible unity of *Brahman* and *Ātman*, experience the unsurpassed bliss of *Brahman*, far superior even to the nectar of the gods.
यथा स्वभावतः क्षारं समुद्रजलं पात्रेण गृहीत्वा पीतञ्चेत्तदा क्षारमेव भवति, तदेव समुद्रजलं मेघैराकृष्य वृष्टं सत् पीतं चेदतिमधुरं भवति; तथा वेदार्थोऽपि स्वातन्त्र्येण गुरुसम्प्रदायं विना विचारितश्चेज्जननमरणदुःखहेतुर्भवति, स एवाद्वैतब्रह्मात्मापरोक्षानुभविश्री सद्गुरुमुखादवाप्यते चेन्नित्यनिरतिशयापरोक्षाखण्डानन्दस्वरूपात्माधिगमाय भवति ।
Just as naturally saline seawater, if scooped up directly in a vessel and drunk, remains only saline, yet that very seawater, drawn up by clouds and showered down as rain, becomes extremely sweet when drunk; so too, if the meaning of the Vedas is investigated independently without Guru-tradition, it becomes the cause of the misery of birth and death, but if it is received from the mouth of a revered true Guru who has direct realization of the non-dual *Brahman*-*Ātman*, it leads to the realization of the *Ātman* whose nature is eternal, unsurpassed, immediate, indivisible *ānanda*.
तस्माद्गुरुमुखादेव वेदार्थो ग्राह्यो वैदिकेन ।
Therefore, the meaning of the Vedas must be grasped by a seeker of the Vedic path only from the lips of the Guru.
113 · ब्रह्मज्ञाद्गुरोरेव वेदार्थो ग्राह्यः
यथा दृतिमुखाद्वा घटमुखाद्वा गृहीतं समुद्रजलं स्वविलक्षणरसास्वादनहेतुर्न भवति, तथाज्ञपुरुषमुखाद्गृहीतं वेदरूपसमुद्रस्यार्थरूपं जलं विलक्षणानन्दजनकं न भवति ।
Just as ocean water drawn through the mouth of a leather vessel or an earthen pot does not become a cause for tasting a distinctly sweet flavor (remaining salty), so too, the water in the form of meaning taken from the ocean of the Veda through the mouth of an unenlightened person does not generate extraordinary *ānanda*.
तस्मादज्ञः पाठकगुरुर्दृतिघटादिसमः ।
Therefore, the ignorant reciter-guru is comparable to a leather bag, an earthen pot, and the like.
ज्ञानी गुरुर्मेघसम इति प्रागुक्तम् ।
That the enlightened guru is like a cloud has been stated earlier.
तस्मान्मुमुक्षुर्दृतिघटादितुल्यमज्ञपाठकगुरुं हित्वा मेघतुल्यब्रह्मज्ञगुरुमुखाद्वेदार्थश्रवणं कुर्यात् तद्विज्ञानार्थं स गुरुमेवाभिगच्छेत् समित्पाणिः श्रोत्रियं ब्रह्मनिष्ठम् (मु. १.२.१२) उपदेक्ष्यन्ति ते ज्ञानं ज्ञानिनस्तत्त्वदर्शिनः (भ. गी. ४.३४) इत्यादिश्रुतिस्मृतिभ्यः ।
Therefore, setting aside an ignorant reciter-guru who is like a leather vessel or earthen pot, a seeker of liberation should perform the hearing of the Vedic import from the mouth of a Brahman-knower guru who is like a cloud, because of Śruti and Smṛti statements such as: To know that, he must approach with sacrificial fuel in hand a guru who is versed in the scriptures and established in Brahman (Mu. 1.2.12) and The wise who have seen the Truth will instruct you in knowledge (Bh. Gītā 4.34).
114 · पौरुषेयप्रबन्धादपि ज्ञानं जायेत
ब्रह्मविन्मुखादेव वेदार्थो ग्रहीतव्य इति पूर्वावर्ते उक्तत्वाद्वेदान्तवाक्यार्थविचारेणैव जीवब्रह्मैक्यज्ञानं जायेत, न त्वितरैः पौरुषेयैः संस्कृतग्रन्थैर्वा भाषाप्रबन्धैर्वेति सिद्धम् ।
Since it was stated in the previous section that the meaning of the Vedas must be grasped only from the mouth of a knower of Brahman, it follows that the knowledge of the oneness of the jīva and Brahman arises only through inquiry into the meaning of the Vedānta texts, and not through other human-authored Sanskrit texts or vernacular treatises.
अस्य प्रबन्धस्य पौरुषेयत्वादारम्भो व्यर्थ इत्याशङ्कायां उच्यते - स यो ह वै तत् परं ब्रह्म वेद ब्रह्मैव1,2
To the doubt that the commencement of this work is futile because it is of human origin, it is replied: 'He who indeed knows that Supreme Brahman becomes Brahman itself...'
टिप्पणी
1.
बिभेत्यल्पश्रुताद्वेदो मामयं प्रतरिष्यति । इतिहासपुराणाभ्यां वेदं समुपबृंहयेत ॥
'The Veda fears a person of little learning, thinking: 'He will distort me.' One should elaborate and reinforce the Veda through the Itihāsas and Purāṇas.'
आचिनोतीति हि शास्त्रमाचारे स्थापयत्यपि । स्वयमाचरते यस्मादाचार्यस्तेन कथ्यते ॥
'Because he gathers the teachings of the scriptures, establishes others in proper conduct, and practices it himself, he is called an Ācārya.'
गुकारो ह्यन्धकारः स्यात् रुकारस्तन्निवर्तकः । ब्रह्मान्धकारनाशित्वाद्गुरुरित्युच्यते बुधैः ॥
'The syllable 'gu' denotes darkness, and the syllable 'ru' is its dispeller. Because he destroys the darkness concealing Brahman, he is called Guru by the wise.'
2.
पौरुषेयवेदान्तप्रकरणग्रन्थैर्ज्ञानं न जायेतेति वदन् वादी प्रष्टव्यः ।
The disputant who claims that knowledge cannot arise from human-authored introductory treatises on Vedānta should be asked:
(१) किं तादृशग्रन्थैर्वेदवचनानुसारित्वाभावान्न ज्ञानं जायेत, (२) उत वेदार्थानुसारित्वाभावाद्वेति ।
(1) Does knowledge fail to arise from such works because they do not literally follow the words of the Veda, or (2) because they do not conform to the meaning of the Veda?
वेदवचनानुसारित्वाभावादिति चेत् - स्मृतीतिहासपुराणादीनामपि तथात्वात्तेभ्योऽपि ज्ञानानुत्पत्तिप्रसङ्गः ।
If you say it is due to not conforming literally to the Vedic words, then since the Smṛtis, Itihāsas, and Purāṇas are also like that, it would absurdly follow that knowledge cannot arise from them either.
अथ वेदार्थानुसारित्वाभावादिति चेत्, तन्न ।
If you argue that it is because they do not conform to the meaning of the Veda, that is not correct.
यथा बहवः स्मृतिपुराणादयो ग्रन्था वेदार्थसारिण एव तथा संस्कृतप्राकृतभाषादिमयवेदान्तप्रकरणग्रन्था अपि बहवो वेदार्थानुसारिणो भवन्त्येव ।
Just as many texts like the Smṛtis and Purāṇas capture the essence of Vedic meaning, likewise many Vedānta treatises composed in Sanskrit, Prākrit, and vernacular languages certainly conform to the Vedic meaning.
यथा चायुर्वेदानुसारिसंस्कृतप्राकृतादिग्रन्था रोगतिन्निवर्तकौषधादिज्ञानं जनयन्ति, तथा वेदार्थानुसारिसंस्कृतप्राकृतादिवेदान्तप्रकरणग्रन्था अपि अद्वितीयात्मविज्ञानं तदुपायज्ञानं च जनयेयुरेव ।
And just as texts in Sanskrit, Prākrit, etc., that follow Āyurveda produce knowledge of diseases and the medicines that cure them, likewise Vedānta treatises in Sanskrit, Prākrit, etc., that follow the Vedic meaning can surely produce knowledge of the non-dual ātman and the means to attain it.
भवति (मु. ३.२.९) इत्यादिश्रुत्या ब्रह्मवित् ज्ञानी ब्रह्मैव ।
According to the Śruti passage, '...becomes [Brahman itself]' (Muṇḍaka 3.2.9), the knower of Brahman is Brahman itself.
अत एव तस्य वाक् वेदरूपैव ।
Therefore, his speech is indeed identical with the Veda.
सा च वाक्, संस्कृतरूपा वास्तु भाषाप्रबन्धादिरूपा वास्तु, सर्वथा तस्य वचनं भेदभ्रमं निवर्तयति निःशेषम् ।
And whether that speech be in Sanskrit or in the form of a vernacular work, his word completely dispels the delusion of difference in every way.
ननु वेदान्तविज्ञानसुनिश्चितार्थाः (मु. ३.२.६) तं त्वौपनिषदं पुरुषम् (बृ. ३.९.२६) इत्यादिश्रुतिभिर्वेदवाक्यादेव ब्रह्मज्ञानं जायेत न तु प्रमाणभूतैरपि प्रबन्धान्तरैरिति चेन्न; तथादर्शनात् ।
If it is objected that according to Śruti passages like 'Those who have well ascertained the truth through the wisdom of Vedānta...' (Muṇḍaka 3.2.6) and 'I ask of that Person known only through the Upaniṣads' (Bṛhadāraṇyaka 3.9.26), knowledge of Brahman arises only from Vedic texts and not from other treatises even if authoritative — we reply: no, because it is observed otherwise in practice.
आयुर्वेदोक्तरोगतन्निदानस्थानौषधादीनां ज्ञानमितरसंस्कृतवैद्यग्रन्थैः प्राकृतभाषादिग्रन्थैश्च यथा सममेवोत्पद्यते, तथा सर्वेषां प्रत्यक्चैतन्याभिन्नाद्वितीयब्रह्मज्ञानमपि पौरुषेयग्रन्थैरपि जायेतैव ।
Just as the knowledge of diseases, diagnosis, etiology, and medicines taught in Āyurveda arises equally well through other Sanskrit medical texts and vernacular books, so too the knowledge of the non-dual Brahman, identical with inner Consciousness, can certainly arise for all through human-authored texts as well.
अत एव सर्वज्ञैर्मुनिभिर्महर्षिभिर्ब्रह्मनिष्ठैः वेदान्तार्थानुवादकैः स्मृतीतिहासपुराणादिबहुग्रन्थैरद्वितीयब्रह्मात्मविद्या स्फुटं विवृता वर्तते ।
That is why the knowledge of the non-dual Brahman-Ātman has been clearly expounded by omniscient sages and great seers established in Brahman through numerous works like the Smṛtis, Itihāsas, and Purāṇas that reiterate the purport of Vedānta.
विनौपनिषदा ज्ञानं चेन्नोत्पद्येत, तदा तैः स्मृत्यादिप्रणयनं व्यर्थं प्रसज्येत ।
If knowledge could not arise except from the Upaniṣadic text directly, then the composition of Smṛtis and other treatises by them would absurdly be rendered futile.
तस्मादुपनिषदामविरोधेनात्मस्वरूपप्रतिपादनपरैर्यैः कैश्चिदपि ग्रन्थैर्ज्ञानं जायेतैवेत्यभ्युपगन्तव्यम् ।
Therefore, it must be accepted that knowledge can certainly arise through any texts whatsoever, provided they expound the true nature of the ātman without contradicting the Upaniṣads.
तत्प्रतिपादकवाक्यानि वैदिकानि वा सन्तु भाषान्तरभूतानि वा, नैवास्ति विशेषः ।
Whether the sentences expounding that truth be Vedic or belonging to other languages, there is no difference whatsoever.
एवञ्च पौरुषेयग्रन्थैरपि ज्ञानं जायेतेति सिद्धम् ।
And thus it is established that knowledge can indeed arise from human-authored works as well.
115 · श्रीगुरुसेवा
यस्य ब्रह्मविदो वाक् वेदवाक्यसमा तमेव ब्रह्मविदमाचार्यबुद्ध्योपासीत जिज्ञासुः ।
The seeker of knowledge should serve with the reverential attitude of a guru that knower of Brahman alone whose words are equal to Vedic declarations.
यदोपासनया गुरुरावर्जितो भवति तदा जिज्ञासुर्निजस्वरूपमपरोक्षीकरोति ।
When the guru is pleased by devotion and service, then the seeker directly realizes his own true nature.
एतेन गुरुसेवाभ्यधिकेश्वरसेवात् इति सिद्ध्यति ।
By this it is established that service to the guru is superior to service to Īśvara.
ईश्वरसेवादृष्टफलमात्रहेतुः, सद्गुरुसेवा तु दृष्टादृष्टोभयफलहेतुः ।
Worship of Īśvara is the cause of unseen results alone, whereas service to the true guru is the cause of both visible and unseen results.
धर्माधर्मोत्पत्तिद्वारा फलहेतुर्यो भवति सोऽदृष्टफलहेतुरिति, धर्माधर्मोत्पत्तिं विनैव साक्षात्फलहेतुर्यो भवति स दृष्टफलहेतुरिति चोच्यते ।
That which becomes the cause of a fruit mediated by the generation of merit or demerit is called 'the cause of an unseen fruit' (adṛṣṭa-phala-hetu), while that which directly causes a fruit without the intermediary of generating merit and demerit is called 'the cause of a seen fruit' (dṛṣṭa-phala-hetu).
ईश्वरसेवा धर्मोत्पत्तिद्वारान्तःकरणशुद्धिरूपफलहेतुर्भवति; अत एवेश्वरसेवादृष्टफलहेतुः ।
Worship of Īśvara becomes the cause of the result consisting in purification of the mind through the generation of merit; therefore, worship of Īśvara is a cause of unseen fruit.
सद्गुरुसेवा तु धर्मनिरपेक्षा आचार्यप्रसादमात्रेणोपदेशात्मकफलहेतुर्भवति ।
Service to the true guru, however, without depending on unseen merit, directly yields the fruit of instruction purely through the grace of the ācārya.
तस्मात् दृष्टफलहेतुः सद्गुरुसेवेत्युच्यते ।
Therefore, service to the true guru is called the cause of visible fruit.
किञ्च सद्गुरुसेवा धर्मोत्पत्तिद्वारान्तःकरणशुद्धिरूपफलहेतुश्चेश्वरसेवावद्भवति ।
Moreover, service to the true guru also serves, just like service to Īśvara, as the cause of the fruit of mental purification through generating merit.
अतोऽदृष्टफलहेतुरपि भवति ।
Hence it is also the cause of unseen fruit.
एतेनाचार्यसेवा हीश्वरोपासनातोऽधिकतरा विज्ञायते ।
Thus, service to the ācārya is recognized as far superior to worship of Īśvara.
तस्मात्सर्वथा जिज्ञासुमुमुक्षुभिः श्रीसद्गुरुवरो ब्रह्मनिष्ठ एव सदा सेव्यः ।
Therefore, by all means, seekers of truth and liberation must always serve only the supreme holy true guru who is established in Brahman.
116 · गुरुसेवाक्रमः
श्रीसद्गुरुलाभोत्तरक्षणे तद्दर्शनमात्रात् सा1
In the very next moment upon gaining the holy true guru, simply on seeing him...
टिप्पणी
1.
पद्भ्यां कराभ्यामूरुभ्यामुरसा शिरसा भुवि । पतनं दीर्घतरुवत् प्रणामोऽष्टाङ्ग उच्यते ॥
Falling prostrate on the ground like a tall tree with both feet, hands, thighs, chest, and head is called eight-limbed prostration (aṣṭāṅga-praṇāma).
पद्भ्यां कराभ्यां जानुभ्यामुरसा शिरसा दृशा । वचसा मनसा चैव प्रणामोऽष्टाङ्ग उच्यते ॥
Prostrating with feet, hands, knees, chest, head, gaze, speech, and mind is also called eight-limbed obeisance.
ष्टाङ्गं नमस्कृत्य तच्चरणकमलरजांसि परिशुद्धतमानि स्वोत्तमाङ्गे धारयेत् ।
Having bowed with eight-limbed prostration, one should place the supremely sacred dust of his lotus feet on one's head.
पश्चात् सद्गुरुप्रसादाद्बन्धमोचनाकाङ्क्षी तीव्रतरमुमुक्षुर्गुरुकुले ब्रह्मचारी वसेत् ।
Thereafter, longing for release from bondage by the grace of the true guru, an intensely earnest seeker of liberation should reside in the guru's abode as a celibate student.
तस्मै स्वशरीरमनोवाग्धनानि समर्पयेत् ।
He should surrender unto him his own body, mind, speech, and wealth.
117 · शरीरार्पणं
श्रीसद्गुरोरभिमतशुश्रूषां बहुतरं कृत्वा मनागपि तदाज्ञानतिलङ्घनेन वर्तनमेव शरीरार्पणमित्युच्यते ।
Performing abundant service agreeable to the holy true guru and conducting oneself without the slightest transgression of his command is called the surrender of the body (śarīrārpaṇa).
118 · मनोऽर्पणं
ब्रह्मविष्णुशिवात्मक एव श्रीसद्गुरुर्नान्य इति मनसा निश्चित्य तस्मिन् परमां भक्तिं प्रेमातिशयं च कृत्वा, यथा तस्य कृपाकटाक्षः स्वस्मिन् पतेत्तथा तमनुवर्तेत ।
Having firmly resolved in mind that the holy true guru is indeed the embodiment of Brahmā, Viṣṇu, and Śiva, and none other, and having cultivated supreme devotion and profound love toward him, one should conduct oneself so that his glance of grace falls upon oneself.
स्वप्नेऽपि तस्मिन् दोषदृष्टिं न कुर्यात् ।
Even in a dream, one should not look for faults in him.
स एव हरिः, हरः, ब्रह्मा, गङ्गा, सूर्यः, इति विजानीयात् ।
One should know that he alone is Hari, Hara, Brahmā, the Gaṅgā, and Sūrya.
निरतिशयक्षेमार्थी मुमुक्षुः श्रीसद्गुरोः स्वरूपं हृदि धारयन् सदा तमेव ध्यायेत् ।
Desiring the unsurpassable highest good, the seeker of liberation should constantly meditate upon him alone, enshrining the true form of the holy true guru in his heart.
एतत्सर्वं मनोऽर्पणमुच्यते ।
All this is called the surrender of the mind (manorpaṇa).
119 · वागर्पणं
यान्यस्माकं सुचरितानि तानि त्वयोपास्यानि (तै. शि. ११-२) इत्यादिश्रुत्युक्तप्रकारेण श्रीसद्गुरुप्रारब्धकर्माधीनशरीरादिगतगुणगणकीर्तनमन्तरा तन्निष्ठदोषाणामनुच्चारणमेव वागर्पणमुच्यते ।
In accordance with the statement of the Śruti, Whatever are our good deeds, those alone should be revered by you (Taittirīya Śikṣāvallī 11.2), never uttering any apparent faults residing in the true guru, but solely praising the host of virtues present in the physical body and personality governed by his prārabdha-karma, is called the surrender of speech (vāgarpaṇa).
120 · धनार्पणं
पत्नीपुत्रभूमिपशुदासीदासद्रव्यगृहधान्यानां धनमिति लोकप्रसिद्धिः ।
Worldly convention designates wife, son, land, cattle, maidservant, manservant, money, house, and grain as 'wealth'.
एतत्सर्वं सन्न्यस्य शरणत्वेन श्रीसद्गुरोरुपसदनमेव धनार्पणमुच्यते ।
Renouncing all this and approaching the holy true Guru as one's refuge is alone called the 'offering of wealth'.
सन्न्यासित्वेन सर्वसङ्गपरित्यागस्य कृतत्वात्कृतार्थः श्रीसद्गुरुर्न धनादिकं किञ्चिदप्यपेक्षेत शिष्येभ्यः ।
Because he has completely abandoned all attachments by virtue of being an ascetic, the fulfilled true Guru expects no wealth or possessions whatsoever from his disciples.
अत एव सर्वपरिग्रहत्यागपूर्वकं स्वार्पणमेव गुरवे धनार्पणमित्युच्यते ।
Therefore, self-surrender unto the Guru, preceded by the renunciation of all worldly possessions, is alone said to be the true 'offering of wealth'.
यदि स्याद्गुरुर्गृहाश्रमी तदा सर्वं धनं तस्मै एव समर्पयेत् ।
If the Guru happens to be a householder, then one should surrender all wealth to him alone.
इमे विदेहाः अयमहमस्मि (बृ. ४.२.४) ।
'Here is the kingdom of Videha [for you], and here am I [at your service]' (Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 4.2.4).
टिप्पणी
1.
सा च भक्तिर्नवविधा - (१) श्रवणं (२) कीर्तनं (३) स्मरणं (४) पादसेवनं (५) अर्चनं (६) वन्दनं (७) दास्यं (८) सख्यं (९) आत्मनिवेदनम्, इति तथा चोक्तमन्यत्र—श्रवणं कीर्तनं चैव स्मरणं पादसेवनम् । अर्चनं वन्दनं दास्यं सख्यमात्मनिवेदनम् इति ।
And that devotion is ninefold: (1) hearing, (2) praising, (3) remembering, (4) serving the feet, (5) worshipping, (6) prostrating, (7) servitude, (8) friendship, and (9) self-surrender; and it has been stated elsewhere: 'Hearing, praising, remembering, serving the feet, worshipping, prostrating, servitude, friendship, and self-surrender.'
2.
श्रीसद्गुरुर्यदा शिष्ये प्रसन्नो भवति तदा स विष्णुरूपेण वर्तते ।
When the holy true Guru is pleased with a disciple, he acts in the form of Viṣṇu.
यदा क्रुद्धस्तदा शिवरूपेण, यदा राजसव्यापारेण युक्तस्तदा ब्रह्मरूपेण, यदा प्रशान्तस्तदा गङ्गारूपेण, यदा वेदान्तवाक्यकरणैः शिष्यभ्रमसंशयादिसहिताज्ञानं निःशेषं निवर्तयति तदा सूर्यरूपेण च वर्तत इति विज्ञेयम् ।
When angry, he acts in the form of Śiva; when engaged in active work, in the form of Brahmā; when fully serene, in the form of the Ganges; and when he entirely dispels the disciple's ignorance along with error, doubt, and the like by means of the statements of Vedānta, he acts in the form of the Sun — thus it should be understood.
इत्थं ब्रह्मनिष्ठे श्रीसद्गुरौ शिष्येण साक्षादीश्वरबुद्धिः कार्या ।
Thus, the disciple should directly maintain the vision of God in the revered true Guru who is established in Brahman.
स्वप्नेऽपि तद्दोषग्राहिणा न भाव्यम् ।
Even in a dream, one should never become one who finds fault in him.
ज्ञानलाभाय ज्ञानावाप्तेः प्राक् गुरुः सेव्यः ।
Prior to attaining knowledge, the Guru is to be served for the sake of obtaining knowledge.
परमपि कृतघ्नतादोषनिवृत्तये गुरुः सेव्यः ।
Even after obtaining knowledge, the Guru is to be served to ward off the blemish of ingratitude.
तथा चोक्तम्—आदौ ज्ञानाप्तये पश्चात् कृतघ्नत्वनिवृत्तये । यावज्जीवं त्रयो वन्द्या वेदान्तो गुरुरीश्वरः इतिवत् ।
And so it is said: 'Initially to obtain knowledge, and afterwards to avoid ingratitude, three are to be venerated for as long as life lasts: Vedānta, the Guru, and God' — just as said here.
अयमपरो धनार्पणप्रकारः ।
This is another manner of offering wealth.
न च ब्रह्मविच्चेद्गुरुः स कथं गृहाश्रमी स्यादिति शङ्क्यम् ।
And it should not be doubted: 'If the Guru is a knower of Brahman, how could he be a householder?'
वसिष्ठविश्वामित्रव्यासयाज्ञवल्क्योद्दालकरामकृष्णादयोऽपि श्रीसद्गुरवो गृहाश्रम एवावर्तन्त इति श्रुतिस्मृतिशतेभ्योऽवगम्यते ।
For it is known from hundreds of Śruti and Smṛti passages that venerable true Gurus like Vasiṣṭha, Viśvāmitra, Vyāsa, Yājñavalkya, Uddālaka, Rāma, and Kṛṣṇa also remained in the householder stage of life.
तस्माद्गृहाश्रमे वर्तमाना अपि ब्रह्मनिष्ठा गुरवो भवेयुः ।
Therefore, even those living in the householder stage who are established in Brahman can be Gurus.
121 · श्रीसद्गुरुलाभानन्तरं शिष्याचरणप्रकारः
निःश्रेयसार्थिना मुमुक्षुणा पूर्वोक्तप्रकारेण शरीरादीन् श्रीसद्गुरवे समर्प्य, तद्वसतावन्यत्र वा गुरोः समीपे वसता, दृढतरात्मापरोक्षसाक्षात्कारपर्यन्तं भिक्षाशिना च भाव्यम् ।
The seeker desiring liberation, having surrendered body and possessions to the revered true Guru in the manner stated previously, dwelling in the Guru's residence or near the Guru elsewhere, should subsist on alms until a very firm direct realization of the ātman is achieved.
ताञ्च भिक्षां स्वातन्त्र्येण न भुञ्जीत, श्रीसद्गुरुसन्निधौ समर्पयेत् ।
And he should not eat that alms-food independently, but should offer it in the presence of the revered true Guru.
श्रीसद्गुरोरनुज्ञा स्याच्चेत् तदाश्नीयाद्भिक्षाम् ।
If there is the permission of the revered true Guru, then he may eat the alms-food.
यदि गुरोः शिष्यभक्त्यतिशयपरीक्षणाय नानुमन्येत तदा तूष्णीमेवोपवसेत् ।
If the Guru does not permit it in order to test the intensity of the disciple's devotion, then he should silently fast.
न चैकस्मिन्दिवसे द्विर्भिक्षार्थं ग्रामं प्रविशेत् ।
And he should not enter the village twice on the same day for alms.
ततः परेद्युर्यथाविधि भिक्षाचर्यं चरेत् ।
Then on the next day he should practice begging alms according to the prescribed rules.
एवं गुरुं सेवमानो न मनागपि मनोविकृतिमाद्येत ।
Thus serving the Guru, he should not incur mental perturbation in the slightest degree.
यदा चिरकालमेवं सेव्यमानो गुरुः प्रसीदति तदा तत्समयं प्रतीक्ष्य स्वजिज्ञासां तस्मै निवेदयेत् ।
When the Guru, being thus served for a long time, becomes pleased, then, awaiting that opportune moment, he should submit his inquiry to him.
स्वामिन्नमस्ते नतलोकबन्धो कारुण्यसिन्धो पतितं भवाब्धौ । मामुद्धरात्मीयकटाक्षदृष्ट्या ऋज्व्यातिकारुण्यसुधाभिवृष्ट्या ॥ (वि. चू. ३७) इत्यादिस्तुतिपूर्वकं प्रश्नेच्छां निवेदयेत् ।
He should express his desire to ask questions preceded by praises such as: O Master, salutations to You, O friend of those who prostrate, O ocean of mercy! Rescue me, who have fallen into the ocean of worldly existence, by the straight shower of the nectar of supreme compassion from Your glance! (Vivekacūḍāmaṇi 37).
यदि पृच्छेत्यनुमतिं दद्यात्तदा स्वसंशयो निवेदनीयः ।
If he gives permission saying 'Ask', then one's doubt should be submitted.
शिष्यस्यानन्तजन्मान्तरार्जितपुण्यपुञ्जवशात् श्रीसद्गुरुः कृपया केवलं धनाद्यनपेक्षया यद्युपदिशेत्, तदोत्तमाधिकारिणस्तस्य निरतिशयकल्याणं जायेत् ।
If, by virtue of the accumulated merit of the disciple across countless past lives, the revered true Guru instructs him purely out of compassion without expectation of wealth or the like, then unsurpassable supreme good (liberation) comes to that highest-qualified aspirant.
तत्रोत्तमोत्तमब्रह्मनिष्ठश्रीसद्गुरुसेवायाः फलं द्विविधम्; श्रीसद्गुरोर्निरतिशयप्रसादलाभः प्रथमं फलम्, द्वितीयं फलं तु कामक्रोधादिसर्वमलनिवृत्तिद्वारान्तःकरणशुद्धिः ।
There, the fruit of serving the highest true Guru established in Brahman is twofold: the first fruit is obtaining the Guru's supreme grace; the second fruit is the purification of the mind by means of the cessation of all impurities such as desire and anger.
एतद्द्वयमप्युत्तमाधिकारिणः शिष्यस्य सिद्धमेव भवति ।
Both of these indeed become established for the highest-qualified disciple.
गुरवे यो मनोवाचौ कायार्थादि समर्प्य तम् । सेवते भवति ब्रह्म प्रसादातिशयाद्गुरोः ॥
Whoever, having surrendered mind, speech, body, wealth, and all else unto the Guru, serves him, becomes Brahman through the supreme grace of the Guru.
यस्तु मनोवाक्कायादिकं श्रीसद्गुरवे समर्प्याहोरात्रं तं शुश्रूषते स हि ब्रह्मैव भवति ।
He who, having surrendered mind, speech, body, and all to the revered true Guru, diligently serves him day and night, surely becomes Brahman alone.
सद्गुरुश्चास्मै साहाय्यं करोति ।1
And the true Guru renders assistance unto him.
टिप्पणी
1.
अयं च नियमो ब्रह्मचारिसन्न्यासिनाम्, न गृहस्थस्य ।
And this rule applies to celibates and renunciants, not to the householder.
